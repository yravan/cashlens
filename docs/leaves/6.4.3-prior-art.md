# 6.4.3 Subscription manager — prior art

Survey for the manager view over 6.4.1's streams: how apps roll a stream up to
an annual cost, what "canceled" means as a user state (vs dismissed, vs a stream
that merely went quiet), how price increases are detected and shown, and what
happens when a canceled service charges again. Sources numbered; decisions at
the end. Surveyed 2026-09-12.

## Commercial apps — the feature in production

1. **Rocket Money — Recurring tab, Inactive list, cancellation concierge**
   (help.rocketmoney.com managing-your-bills-and-subscriptions,
   missing-subscriptions, subscription-cancellation-faqs,
   why-can-t-rocket-money-cancel-this; rocketmoney.com/learn
   tracking-expenses-with-rocket-money): the reference product for this leaf's
   wording. "Any subscriptions or bills without detected payments in the past
   month will appear in the Inactive list at the bottom"; "when our system
   detects a new payment for that bill, it will automatically move back to your
   Active list" — inactivity is *derived from the ledger*, never a user
   decision. Cancellation is a concierge service (2.5M cancellations claimed)
   with a support path for "if you were charged after using Rocket Money to
   cancel a subscription" — being charged after a cancel is a first-class
   support scenario, not an edge case. Price-increase alerts are marketed
   ("if a service quietly raises their pricing, you'll get notified before the
   next charge hits") with no published threshold. No annual figure is shown in
   the help articles; the list is monthly-first.
2. **Copilot Money — Pause vs Archive** (help.copilot.money
   pausing-and-archiving-recurrings, editing-recurrings, creating-recurrings):
   two distinct user states. *Pause* is temporary — the item leaves "expected
   spend" and "the next time Copilot receives a payment for a paused Recurring,
   it will automatically be marked as active again". *Archive* is permanent —
   "if you cancel a gym membership, then you can archive the Recurring
   transaction data for historical reference"; the expense is "permanently
   removed from your expected spend" and history is kept. The article never
   says what happens when an archived recurring charges again. Amounts are
   filter ranges ("from $9 to $10"), so a price change is handled by editing the
   filter — no automatic detection.
3. **Monarch Money — Active / Canceled merchant status, yellow amount dot**
   (help.monarch.com Tracking-Recurring-Expenses-and-Bills,
   Getting-Started-with-Bill-Sync): the merchant list is "sorted by Active and
   Canceled"; the recommended cancel path is "Edit merchant details, then switch
   the Recurring status from Active to Canceled" — explicitly "to ensure you
   don't lose any history", contrasted with "Mark merchant as not recurring"
   (our *dismissed*). Two separate intents: *not recurring* (detection was
   wrong) and *canceled* (detection was right, the service ended). The calendar
   marks "paid, but at a different amount than expected" with a yellow dot —
   amount deviation is surfaced per occurrence, direction-agnostic, no
   threshold published. Every sync re-scans for recurring items; nothing in the
   docs says a canceled merchant auto-reactivates on a new charge.
4. **Emma — committed spend and price-increase alerts**
   (emma-app.com/features/recurring-payments; App Store reviews; pennywisefinance
   review): "Get real-time alerts when subscription prices increase" plus a
   "committed spend" view. Reviewers report the detection groups un-cancelable
   bills (water) with subscriptions (Netflix) and that alert volume leads
   people to switch notifications off — a persistent in-list marker beats a
   notification stream for a ledger-first product.
5. **Snoop and Monzo — bill-hike detection at the bank**
   (snoop.app; getsmartsaver.co.uk snoop-uk-review-2026; monzo.com/blog
   2018/07/04 making-recurring-payments-better): Snoop's headline feature is
   "notify you of any price hikes" on bills; Monzo predicts recurring payments
   "based on the amount you were last charged" and its Bill Tracker "tells you
   when a regular bill changes". Both compare the *latest* charge against
   history — retrospective by construction (Finny/KeepMySubs: "every app in this
   category is retrospective: it compares the new charge against the previous
   one... you have already paid the higher price at least once").
6. **Bobby — manual tracker, per-period totals, per-currency breakdown**
   (App Store id1059152023; hulry.com track-subscriptions; idownloadblog 2019):
   tapping the total toggles weekly / monthly / yearly; the yearly figure is the
   number reviewers call "the indicator of whether I'm spending too much".
   Multi-currency totals are shown *as a breakdown by currency* first, with
   optional FX conversion into a base currency (daily rates) — the breakdown is
   what makes foreign subscriptions legible.
7. **Subby — mark as cancelled, hide what you no longer pay**
   (play.google.com com.slapp.subby; 19pine.ai roundup): the cancel flow is
   explicit — "cancel directly with the service provider, then mark as
   cancelled in Subby to stop future reminders". A recurring user complaint is
   wanting to "hide subscriptions I am no longer paying for" because canceled
   items clutter the active view — canceled needs its own place, out of the
   main list and out of totals.
8. **Truebill / Rocket Money and Trim — what goes wrong after "canceled"**
   (bbb.org Rocket Money complaints; ramseysolutions.com what-is-rocket-money;
   complaintsboard.com rocket-money; epic.org EPIC CFPB complaint 2022;
   finmasters.com trim-review): the dominant complaint class is "continued
   charges after cancellation" — both the app's own membership and third-party
   subscriptions the concierge reported as canceled ("I canceled my Hulu
   subscription through the true bill app... it still said the same thing... I
   had to go back and cancel within seconds myself"). Provider-side
   cancellation is a liability surface (credentials, concierge, refunds, a CFPB
   dark-patterns complaint); the detectable, honest signal is *a charge posting
   after the user said they canceled*.

## Open source

9. **Wallos** (github.com/ellite/Wallos CHANGELOG): "allow to disable
   subscriptions" (1.5.0), "add stats about inactive subscriptions" (1.6.0),
   "sort disabled subscription at the bottom" (1.6.0), "option to hide disabled
   subscriptions" (1.22.0), "replacement for disabled subscriptions, to more
   accurately calculate savings" (2.33.0), "store weekly the total yearly cost of
   subscriptions" (2.41.0), and two bug fixes — "next payment date not updating
   for disabled subscriptions" (1.17.3) then "don't update next payment date for
   disabled subscriptions" (2.42.0) — the projection must simply stop for a
   disabled item, not keep advancing. No price-change or price-history feature
   in 185+ releases. Multi-currency is converted through Fixer.io into a main
   currency.
10. **SubTrackr** (github.com/bscott/subtrackr README, subtrackr.xyz): "total
    monthly/yearly spend" on the dashboard; README limitation: "SubTrackr
    currently tracks only the current price per subscription" — no price
    history, so no increase detection. Multi-currency via Fixer with EUR as the
    forced base.
11. **Actual Budget — schedules** (actualbudget.org/docs/schedules; issue
    #4098): "is approximately" matches "plus/minus 7.5% of the amount entered"
    — the tolerance 6.4.1 already adopted as its stability band; "is between"
    for explicit ranges. Issue #4098 asks for schedule templates that budget a
    fixed "+10%" because "costs increase by x% per year" — users expect the
    tool to acknowledge upward drift explicitly.
12. **Firefly III — bills** (docs.firefly-iii.org how-to subscriptions,
    explanation subscriptions; issues #991, #1406, #2985): a bill's min/max
    amounts are *expectation only* — "the actual amount of a transaction linked
    to this subscription is always allowed to be more or less"; the average is
    the expected amount in charts. Linking is done by rules, and editing the
    bill's range does not update the rule (#2985), so a price increase silently
    stops matching — #991 asks for "your bill matched the name... but the amount
    is outside of the set range" prompts. Lesson: a fixed range is the wrong
    detector for hikes; compare the latest charge to the stream's own history
    instead.
13. **Lunch Money — end date as the cancel mechanism**
    (support.lunchmoney.app finances/recurring-items the-basics-of-recurring,
    revising-recurring-items): a recurring item's "active period" has an
    optional end date — "if you terminated your Netflix subscription in June
    2023, set the end date... the recurring item will not show up in July 2023
    or later"; past links stay attached after edits. Status per occurrence:
    linked (check), expected date passed with nothing linked (warning), future
    (clock).
14. **YNAB — scheduled transactions** (support.ynab.com
    editing-and-deleting-scheduled-transactions): a repeating transaction is
    ended by deleting "all future repeating scheduled ones"; nothing is inferred
    from the ledger — the user owns the schedule and its amount, and a price
    change is a manual edit.

## Provider contract and platform rules

15. **Plaid Recurring Transactions — `TransactionStream`** (plaid-java
    `TransactionStream.java`, `TransactionStreamStatus.java`;
    plaid.com/docs/transactions; plaid.com/blog/recurring-transactions): streams
    carry both `average_amount` and `last_amount` — the two numbers whose
    divergence *is* a price change — plus `is_active` ("indicates whether the
    transaction stream is still live") and a nullable `predicted_next_date`.
    `status` is `MATURE` ("at least 3 transactions... For Annual recurring
    stream, we will mark it MATURE after 2 instances"), `EARLY_DETECTION`, or
    `TOMBSTONED` ("no further transactions were found at the next expected
    date"). `is_user_modified`: "As the ability to modify transactions streams
    has been discontinued, this field will always be false" — Plaid tried
    user-editable streams and withdrew them; user intent belongs in the
    consuming app's own table, which is where 6.4.1 put it.
16. **Apple App Store — price-increase consent thresholds**
    (support.apple.com/109501; developer.apple.com App Store Connect
    auto-renewable-subscription-price-increase-thresholds): a developer may
    raise a price without re-consent only if the increase "does not exceed
    approximately USD $5 and 50% of the subscription price, or USD $50 and 50%
    for annual subscriptions", at most once per year, with a per-country table
    of absolute thresholds "subject to change based on changes in tax or foreign
    exchange rates". The platform's own rule is a *conjunction of an absolute and
    a percentage*, currency-specific and maintained by a pricing team — a table
    this product cannot own without an FX source.
17. **KeepMySubs / Hiatus / Subkept — retrospective comparison, default 10%**
    (keepmysubs.com/blog/price-hike-alerts; getfinny.app apps-track-
    subscription-price-increases-2026): every price-hike tool "compares each
    new charge to the last one"; KeepMySubs alerts "when a new charge comes in
    past your alert threshold (10% by default) above the previous one" and
    "shows the before and after amounts and points to the exact charge that
    triggered it". None compares against an average, none uses an absolute
    threshold, none addresses variable bills.

## Decisions

**Adopted**
- **Annual cost per stream and per-currency totals, never converted** (6, 9,
  10, 16): the yearly figure is the one users reach for; it is `typical ×
  cycles per year` (weekly 52, biweekly 26, monthly 12, annual 1), integer
  minor units, sign preserved. Totals are per currency with outflows and
  inflows kept apart (the 6.4.2 rule), shown as a breakdown like Bobby's — no
  FX (Wallos/SubTrackr need a Fixer key and a base currency; repo law forbids
  combining currencies).
- **`canceled` as a third persisted status, distinct from `dismissed`** (2, 3,
  7, 13): Monarch's Canceled-vs-not-recurring and Copilot's Archive-vs-Pause
  are the same split — *dismissed* means the detection was wrong, *canceled*
  means it was right and the service ended. Canceled keeps history (Monarch
  "don't lose any history", Copilot "for historical reference", Lunch Money's
  links survive) under its own heading, out of the main list and out of every
  total (Subby's clutter complaint, Wallos's hide/sort-to-bottom evolution).
- **Canceled streams leave the projection entirely** (2, 9, 13): Copilot
  archived items leave expected spend permanently; Wallos twice fixed "next
  payment date updating for disabled subscriptions"; Lunch Money's end date
  stops the item from appearing in later months. `projectUpcoming` skips
  canceled exactly like dismissed — not even in "Gone quiet", which is the
  derived-inactivity state (Rocket's Inactive, Plaid's `is_active`) for streams
  nobody has ruled on.
- **"Charged after you marked it canceled" as a per-stream marker** (1, 8):
  the honest signal the concierge apps' complaint threads are about. A canceled
  stream whose latest posted occurrence is dated after the day the decision
  was recorded is flagged with both dates; the user either confirms it is
  back ("It's back" → confirmed) or restates the cancel ("Still canceled",
  which re-stamps the decision day). Nothing auto-reactivates (Copilot documents
  auto-reactivation only for *paused*; Monarch is silent) — a charge after a
  cancel is exactly the thing the user must see, not something to silently
  undo.
- **Price increase = latest charge above the stream's own typical, beyond the
  7.5% band, outflows only** (3, 5, 11, 15, 17): compare `last_amount` to
  `average_amount` (Plaid's two fields; 6.4.1's `lastAmountMinor` vs
  `typicalAmountMinor`), retrospective like every shipping detector. The
  tolerance reuses the detector's own STABLE band (Actual's 7.5%) via the same
  exact-rational compare — a high-confidence stream is amount-stable by
  definition and can never be flagged, so the marker only ever appears on
  streams whose history genuinely moved. Direction matters: a bigger paycheck
  is not a price increase. Shown as a persistent in-row marker with both
  amounts (KeepMySubs' "before and after"), never a notification (Emma's
  "notification bombard").
- **Decision day from the decision row's own `updated_at`, no new column**
  (least data): `setRecurringStatus` already stamps `updated_at = now()` on
  every post; the UTC day of that stamp is "when you marked it canceled".
  Re-posting `canceled` re-stamps, which is the "Still canceled" action for
  free.

**Rejected**
- **Provider-side cancellation, concierge, negotiation** (1, 8): a credential
  and liability surface (CFPB complaint, refund disputes) with no ledger truth
  behind it; this leaf records intent and watches the ledger.
- **Auto-reactivating a canceled stream on a new charge** (1, 2): Rocket's
  Inactive→Active flip is for a *derived* state; flipping a *user decision*
  silently would erase the one fact the user needs to act on.
- **Absolute or per-currency price thresholds** (16, 17): Apple's "$5 and 50%"
  is a maintained per-country table; KeepMySubs' 10% is arbitrary. A relative
  band the detector already owns is exact, currency-agnostic, and needs no
  table.
- **Amount ranges as the detector** (2, 12): Copilot filters and Firefly bill
  ranges make a hike *stop matching* rather than get highlighted; the
  detector's history-relative band is the right tool.
- **Notifications, reminders, renewal alerts** (1, 4, 9): explicitly out of
  scope for this leaf; the page is the surface.
- **FX conversion to a base currency** (6, 9, 10): no FX source in the repo;
  per-currency breakdown is the honest figure.
- **Price history table / per-occurrence yellow dots** (3, 17): the detector
  recomputes from posted rows on every read, so the ledger is the history;
  per-occurrence deviation flags are 6.5.2 (amount anomalies) territory.
- **Storing a separate `canceled_on` day** (13's end date): a real column would
  allow backdating, but the leaf's question is "did it charge after I told the
  app it was canceled", which the decision stamp answers with no new data.
- **Fixing >25% jumps at the detector** (11, 12): 6.4.1's 25% acceptance band
  ejects a single much-higher charge (a 40% hike is ejected until a third
  new-price charge lands and the median moves), so a large hike shows as a
  stalled stream, then "Gone quiet", before it re-emerges — a detector
  level-shift rule is a 6.4.1/6.5.2 follow-up, named as a deferral, not built
  here.
