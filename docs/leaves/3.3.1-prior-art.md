# 3.3.1 Internal Transfer Matching — Prior Art

Survey date: 2026-09-05

Scope: how production PFM apps and open-source ledgers detect transfers between a user's own accounts (credit-card payments, savings moves); the amount/date-window/account-pair heuristics and their false-positive management; how pairs are presented and corrected; whether transfers are auto-excluded from spend; what signals Plaid itself carries; why pending rows are unstable ground.

## Sources and decisions

| # | Source | Evidence | Cash Lens decision |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Maybe Finance `Family::AutoTransferMatchable`](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/blob/main/app/models/family/auto_transfer_matchable.rb) | Production-grade open-source matcher, one SQL self-join: opposite signs, `inflow.account_id <> outflow.account_id`, both accounts in the same family, date window `inflow.date BETWEEN outflow.date - 4 AND outflow.date + 4`, same-currency pairs require exact inverse amounts (cross-currency allowed at ±5% via FX rates); LEFT JOIN on `transfers` excludes pairs where *either* side is already matched; candidates ordered `date_diff ASC`, then Ruby greedily claims pairs via a used-id set so each transaction lands in at most one pair. | Adopt the whole shape: equal-magnitude opposite-sign, different accounts, same user, symmetric ±4-day window, already-paired exclusion, greedy date-proximity claiming with a used set. Tighten the ordering: `date_diff ASC` alone leaves ties nondeterministic — add total tiebreakers (outflow date, then ids) so re-runs are byte-stable. Reject cross-currency FX-tolerance pairs: no FX-rate source in this codebase, and a ±5% band is a guess presented as fact — cross-currency transfers stay unmatched (named deferral). |
| 2 | [Maybe PR #1690 — RejectedTransfer model](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/1690) | They first modeled rejection as `Transfer.status='rejected'`, then ripped it out: a transaction could belong to multiple transfer rows and sync-time filtering became "confusing and error prone"; a rejected transfer is "equivalent to 'no transfer'". The replacement is a standalone `RejectedTransfer` (inflow_id, outflow_id) exclusion list consulted during auto-matching, so a declined pair is never re-proposed. | Adopt the negative-memory pattern, but as one table, not two: a `transfer_pairs` row with `dismissed_at` set is exactly their RejectedTransfer, and active-pair uniqueness is enforced by partial indexes `WHERE dismissed_at IS NULL`. One table keeps the FK/RLS/grant surface half the size, and the full combo-unique doubles as the exclusion key. |
| 3 | [Maybe issue #1602](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/issues/1602) + [PR #1585](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/1585) | Bug report: auto-matching grouped an inflow dated *before* its outflow; initially "fixed" by requiring outflow-first, then reverted (#1661, shipped with #1690): loan/card providers routinely post the receiving side a day or two before the sending bank clears it, so the window must be symmetric. | Adopt the symmetric window — the receiving half may post before the sending half. The seed's card-payment pair (checking 03-05 → card 03-07) exercises one direction; unit tests cover both. |
| 4 | [Monarch: Transfers & Credit Card Payments](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393292-Transfers-and-Credit-Card-Payments) + [coaching-site correction workflow](https://evolvingmoneycoaching.com/avoid-these-mistakes-in-monarch-money-and-an-explanation-on-transfers/) | Anything in the "Transfers" category group (Transfer, Credit Card Payment, Buy, Sell) is excluded from budget/cash-flow/spending "so that it is not double counted"; the card *purchase* counts as spend, the later payment never does. Known failure mode: a Zelle/Venmo payment *to another person* auto-labeled Transfer silently inflates savings; the documented correction is manual recategorization — a purely categorical model has no pair to inspect or unlink. | Adopt the exclusion contract (this leaf's reason to exist — 6.1.1 reads pair state). Reject the category-only mechanism: a pair link (two rows, two accounts, provable zero-sum) is structural truth a category label can't fake; one-sided payments to other people fail the counterpart test instead of silently vanishing from spend. |
| 5 | [YNAB: Credit Card Payments, Now Smarter](https://www.ynab.com/blog/credit-card-payments-now-smarter) + [Approving & Matching guide](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/approving-and-matching-transactions-a-guide-ByYNZaQ1i) + [transfers guide](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/transfer-transactions-a-guide-HJOsZz4Jj) | When both sides of a card payment import "within a few days of each other and the amounts line up", YNAB links them automatically — no payee rule required. They name the false positive honestly: "a $10 refund on your card and a $10 transfer to a friend are the same number"; payee rules assist, and ambiguous payees fall back to amount-only. Matches surface for approval and can be unmatched. Philosophy: transfers between on-budget accounts carry no category — money is only categorized when it enters or leaves the budget, not when moved between pockets. | Adopt "within a few days + amounts line up" as the industry-converged core rule, and the amount-collision honesty — pairs render as visible, unlinkable markers, never silent exclusions. Adopt the no-category philosophy in reversible form: actively paired rows leave the 4.2.1 auto-categorize queue; unlinking re-admits them. Reject payee-string rules here (4.2.2's territory; bank transfer strings are institution-specific). |
| 6 | [Actual Budget: Transfers](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/transfers/) + [Identify & Apply Transfers Historically](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/scripts/modify-transfers/) | Native transfers are linked payee pairs (edit one side, the other follows). For already-imported two-sided data, their official script pairs rows where "the amounts are exactly the same but inverted", "the transaction dates are within 3 days of each other", and "the match only occurs once". Import guidance: without an explicit link, reports double-count. | Adopt exact-inverse + small window + match-once (their 3 days and Maybe's 4 bracket the choice; 4 wins — see Distilled design). Reject the linked-payee mutation model (edits mirroring across sides): Cash Lens rows are provider-owned facts; the pair is metadata over them, so re-syncs never fight user edits. |
| 7 | [Firefly III discussions #1345](https://github.com/firefly-iii/firefly-iii/issues/1345) / [#10191 + maintainer position](https://github.com/orgs/firefly-iii/discussions/10191) + [transfer import docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/how-to/data-importer/import/transfers/) | Firefly models a transfer as ONE double-entry row, so two-sided imports produce a withdrawal + deposit it cannot merge; a maintainer calls automatic pair-detection-and-merge "pretty complex" and unshipped; users pre-process with scripts matching dates/amounts/descriptions. | Reject the merge-into-one-row model: Cash Lens keeps both provider rows immutable (provenance, per-account balance math, re-sync idempotence) and records the pair as a separate link row — which is what makes auto-matching *tractable* where Firefly finds it complex (no destructive merge, trivially reversible). |

| 16 | [Plaid: pending transactions lifecycle](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/) | A pending transaction posts as a **new** `transaction_id` while the pending row is **removed**; name and amount may change in between (tips); the posted date differs; some pendings (auth holds) never post at all; `pending_transaction_id` linkage is best-effort and null at some institutions. | Adopt posted-only matching: a pair anchored to a pending row is built on an id that is *scheduled to be deleted* and an amount that may still change — it would dissolve on posting and re-form days later, pure churn (and 3.2.1 pending↔posted reconciliation is explicitly not this leaf). The delete-cascade unpair already handles any paired row that later vanishes, so posted-only costs nothing in safety. |

## Distilled design (what 3.3.1 builds)

1. **The rule** (#1, #3, #5, #6, #13): two posted transactions pair when they belong to the same user in different accounts, carry the same currency, have exactly inverse non-zero minor amounts (one strictly negative, one strictly positive), and their dates are at most **4 days** apart in either direction. Four days is the widest production-evidenced window (Maybe ±4; Actual 3; YNAB "a few days"; Quicken "about the same date") and card payments need the width (ACH pull + posting lag; the seed's card pair spans 2 days). Symmetric because receivers post first at real institutions (#3).
2. **Determinism & one-to-one** (#1, #6, #13): candidate edges are sorted by (date distance, outflow date, outflow id, inflow id) and claimed greedily with a used-set — every transaction in at most one active pair, closest-dated pairs win, ties broken totally, so re-runs and shuffled inputs produce identical pairs. The exhaustive unit suite lives here.
3. **Schema** (#2, #7, #13): a `transfer_pairs` link row (outflow txn, inflow txn, user, `dismissed_at`) over immutable transaction rows — never a merge, never row mutation. Composite `(transaction_id, user_id)` FKs with `ON DELETE CASCADE` make the 2.1.4 removed-transaction path unpair automatically; partial unique indexes (`WHERE dismissed_at IS NULL`) enforce one active pair per transaction *in the database*, not just in code; the full `(outflow, inflow)` unique is the dismissal memory key. RLS forced, own-row policies, app-role grants least-privilege (no UPDATE on identity columns).
4. **Unlink = lossless negative memory** (#2, #8, #13): "Not a transfer" stamps `dismissed_at` on the link row — both transactions untouched (Quicken's community begged for exactly this), the combination never re-proposed, and each half stays free to pair with a different counterpart. No confirm-first gate (#13 rationale); missed-pair manual linking is deferred to the review queue (5.1/5.4).
5. **Self-healing, idempotent runs** (#1, #13, #16): one matching pass = dissolve active pairs whose halves no longer satisfy the rule (re-synced amounts/dates/status can drift), then form new pairs among unpaired eligible rows, all in one RLS-scoped transaction, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` absorbing concurrent runs. Triggers per repo precedent: inline after a committed sync that changed rows (both halves often land in different runs — #13c), and on transactions-page visit (covers seeded/manual/import data; no cron by design — RLS forbids cross-user sweeps).
6. **Presentation** (#4, #5, #8, #10): a textual per-row marker naming the counterpart account ("Transfer · to Rainy Day Savings"), never color-only, with the one correction affordance the survey demands: Unlink. Spend-exclusion is the contract this hands to 6.1.1 (paired ⇒ out of true spend), matching every surveyed product (#4, #8, #10, #11, #14).
7. **Category interplay** (#5, #8): actively paired rows leave the 4.2.1 auto-categorize queue (both the batch select and the write predicate — the same lost-race guard 4.2.1 already uses), because a transfer is not spend and burning LLM tokens to label it invites the Monarch/Mint failure mode. Reversible by construction: unlink re-admits the rows on the next visit. Rows categorized *before* pairing keep their category — pairing is flow-truth, categories stay user property.
8. **Pendings stay out** (#16): only posted rows enter the matcher; a pending half pairs on the visit after it posts.
