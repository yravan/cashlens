# Leaf 3.5.1 — Scheduled obligations: prior art

Research date: 2026-09-14

## Scope examined

> **3.5.1 Scheduled obligations** `[P1]` — Record known future charges — rent, insurance, tuition — so they appear in upcoming views before any bank sees them.

The adjacent leaf is a hard boundary:

> **3.5.2 Expectation matching** `[P2]` — When a scheduled charge arrives, link it to its obligation automatically; alert when it's overdue or the amount is wrong.

This survey therefore looks for the smallest reliable way to declare and project a future charge. Matching an imported transaction, deciding that an occurrence was paid, amount-variance detection, and overdue alerts are evidence for the next leaf, not features to pull into this one.

## Sources surveyed (21)

### 1. YNAB — scheduled transactions and import matching

**Sources:** [Scheduled Transactions in YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/scheduled-transactions-a-guide-BygrAIFA9), [Approving and Matching Transactions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/approving-and-matching-transactions-a-guide-ByYNZaQ1i), [duplicate-import troubleshooting](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/my-transactions-are-importing-more-than-once-ByyZUaPge)

**What it does.** YNAB lets a person enter a future-dated transaction and repeat it. Scheduled entries appear ahead of time and imported transactions are later expected to match the manual/scheduled entry. It also documents manual matching and duplicate recovery because automatic matching is not infallible.

**Adopt.** Make a known future charge an explicit user declaration; support both one-time and small, familiar repeating schedules; show it before the bank posts anything.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not insert a future ledger transaction and later hope an import merges into it. Do not implement matching or duplicate repair in this leaf.

### 2. Monarch Money — recurring bills calendar

**Source:** [Tracking Recurring Expenses and Bills](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/4890751141908-Tracking-Recurring-Expenses-and-Bills)

**What it does.** Monarch detects recurring merchants, permits manual recurring-item management, and presents upcoming bills in list and calendar forms.

**Adopt.** Put declarations into the existing Upcoming surface and distinguish known items from detected predictions with visible source labels.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not turn this into a broad subscriptions center or infer a declaration from merchant history; Cash Lens already owns detection in 6.4.1.

### 3. Copilot Money — editable recurrings and “left to pay”

**Sources:** [Recurrings overview](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/9778259-recurrings-tab-overview), [Creating Recurrings](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/3760068-creating-recurrings), [Editing Recurrings](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/3783837-editing-recurrings), [Pausing and Archiving Recurrings](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/3983286-pausing-and-archiving-recurrings)

**What it does.** Copilot shows what is expected in the current month, what remains, and less-frequent future items. A person can create, edit, pause, or archive recurrings and change dates, amounts, and frequency.

**Adopt.** Provide an understandable active lifecycle—add, edit, end—and keep the monthly “still to leave” framing already used by Cash Lens.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not add pause/archive variants, analytics, or history-based association rules before the basic declaration works.

### 4. Rocket Money — Upcoming, All, and Calendar views

**Sources:** [Where to view subscriptions and bills](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/3117398-where-can-i-view-my-subscriptions-and-bills), [Managing bills and subscriptions](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2185531-managing-your-bills-and-subscriptions)

**What it does.** Rocket Money detects regular charges, allows manual bill management, and offers upcoming, all, and calendar views ordered by expected charge date.

**Adopt.** Preserve both chronological and calendar presentations; keep management near the forecast rather than adding another top-level destination.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not add provider-side cancellation, subscription negotiation, or a second recurring-detection model.

### 5. Quicken and Simplifi — reminders as forecast inputs

**Sources:** [About reminders](https://info.quicken.com/win/about-reminders), [Project account balances](https://info.quicken.com/win/project-your-account-balances-personal), [Projected balances on Mac](https://info.quicken.com/mac/projected-balances), [Simplifi Bills & Income](https://support.simplifi.quicken.com/en/articles/4109588-using-the-bills-income-section)

**What it does.** Quicken models future bills, income, and transfers as reminders and combines them with register data for cash-flow and account-balance forecasts.

**Adopt.** Treat an obligation as an input to a forecast, not as historical fact. Binding it to an account is a proven way to make the declaration concrete.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Account-balance curves, income schedules, transfers, bill payment, and reminder notifications exceed “appear in upcoming views.”

### 6. Actual Budget — schedules

**Sources:** [Schedules](https://actualbudget.org/docs/schedules/), [Scheduled Transactions tour](https://actualbudget.org/docs/tour/schedules/)

**What it does.** Actual supports one-off and repeating schedules, several cadence patterns, exact/approximate/ranged amounts, weekend shifting, configurable look-ahead, manual approval or automatic posting, and matching near the expected date. Its day-31 recurrence skips months without that date.

**Adopt.** Use a first date, a limited cadence, and an optional inclusive end date. Project only through the same bounded horizon as the current Upcoming page.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not post transactions, add approximate/ranged amounts, shift weekends, support multiple days per month, or match within a date/amount tolerance. Do not copy its day-31 skip semantics because Cash Lens already preserves the original anchor and clamps to month end.

### 7. Actual Budget — duplicate failure and explicit merge repair

**Sources:** [Issue #669: imported transaction does not match entered transaction](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/669), [25.5.0 manual duplicate merging](https://actualbudget.org/blog/release-25.5.0/), [Merge transactions documentation](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/merging/), [Issue #3762: schedule matching edge cases](https://github.com/actualbudget/actual/issues/3762)

**What it does.** Actual users reported imported rows failing to match manually entered or scheduled rows and creating duplicates. The product later added explicit merging; its documented merge keeps the synced transaction and fills missing information from the manual one.

**Adopt.** Keep projections out of the ledger, preserve source identity, and make any potential overlap visible rather than silently treating a guess as identity proof.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not materialize scheduled rows, fuzzy-merge imports, or ship a repair workflow. Those are exactly the failure modes and responsibilities assigned to 3.5.2.

### 8. Firefly III — recurring transactions versus subscriptions

**Sources:** [Recurring transactions](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/explanation/financial-concepts/recurring/), [Subscriptions](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/explanation/financial-concepts/subscriptions/)

**What it does.** Firefly III separates rules that generate recurring transactions from subscriptions that describe expected bills. The distinction acknowledges that an expectation and a booked ledger event are not the same fact.

**Adopt.** Preserve three concepts in Cash Lens: detected pattern, explicit declaration, actual transaction.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not generate ledger transactions from a schedule or recreate both of Firefly’s larger subsystems.

### 9. GnuCash — scheduled transaction templates

**Sources:** [Scheduling Transactions](https://www.gnucash.org/docs/v5/C/gnucash-manual/trans-sched.html), [Scheduled Transaction editor](https://www.gnucash.org/docs/v5/C/gnucash-manual/sched-editor.html), [developer model](https://code.gnucash.org/docs/STABLE/group__SchedXaction.html)

**What it does.** GnuCash stores transaction templates with start/end controls, recurrence, occurrence limits, reminders/advance creation, and optional weekend adjustment.

**Adopt.** Account-bound declarations, explicit schedule bounds, and an editable lifecycle are mature accounting patterns.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not expose split templates, formulas, arbitrary intervals, weekend policy, occurrence counters, or automatic transaction creation.

### 10. Lunch Money — recurring items and missing occurrences

**Sources:** [Recurring-item basics](https://support.lunchmoney.app/finances/recurring-items/the-basics-of-recurring), [Creating recurring items](https://support.lunchmoney.app/finances/recurring-items/creating-recurring-items), [Overview projections](https://support.lunchmoney.app/home/overview), [v2 migration/API concepts](https://alpha.lunchmoney.dev/v2/migration-guide)

**What it does.** Lunch Money represents predictable expenses or income as recurring items, projects remaining recurring amounts, and exposes expected and missing occurrence dates separately from transactions.

**Adopt.** Generate occurrence views from one declaration and keep occurrence dates virtual. A passive “scheduled date passed” state can be distinct from a transaction.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not claim an item is paid, missing, or matched without the evidence model that 3.5.2 will add; do not add expected income to a leaf scoped to charges.

### 11. Buxfer — reminders included in forecasts

**Sources:** [Forecast help](https://www.buxfer.com/help/forecast), [future balances and expenses](https://www.buxfer.com/features/forecast/preview-future-balances-expenses-cashflow), [Reminder API](https://www.buxfer.com/help/api)

**What it does.** Buxfer includes future-dated transactions and reminders in forecasts and recommends reminders for obligatory recurring expenses such as rent and utilities. Its reminder API carries a name, start date, recurrence period, amount, and account.

**Adopt.** The minimal fields—name, amount/currency, account, first date, recurrence—directly fit the leaf.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not expand into long-range balance simulation or a public reminder API.

### 12. Tiller community templates — future rows and reconciliation

**Sources:** [Scheduled Transactions template](https://community.tiller.com/t/scheduled-transactions-template-project-future-account-balances-calculate-credit-card-payments-auto-reconcile-transactions/19973), [Projected Balances sheet](https://community.tiller.com/t/docs-projected-balances-sheet/373), [Expense forecast calendar](https://community.tiller.com/t/expense-forecasting-and-seeing-upcoming-expenses-on-a-calendar-updated-4-15-24/25118)

**What it does.** Community spreadsheets generate future rows to project balances, calendarize expenses, and reconcile generated rows with later downloads.

**Adopt.** The demand is real: people want known commitments mixed into an upcoming calendar before download.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Generated ledger rows and auto-reconciliation make duplicate correctness depend on matching. Cash Lens should project on read instead.

### 13. Ledger CLI — periodic transactions under forecast mode

**Sources:** [Ledger 3 manual: periodic transactions and forecasting](https://ledger-cli.org/doc/ledger3.html), [`ledger(1)` forecast options](https://ledger-cli.org/doc/ledger.1.html)

**What it does.** Ledger defines periodic transaction templates but expands them only for reports run with forecast options. The journal remains unchanged.

**Adopt.** Read-time virtual expansion is the cleanest precedent for a ledger-first product: declarations affect a forecast, never historical truth.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not expose a period-expression DSL or double-entry template language.

### 14. Beancount Beanlabs forecast plugin

**Sources:** [forecast.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beancount/beanlabs/master/beanlabs/plugins/forecast.py), [Beanlabs stability warning](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beancount/beanlabs/master/README.md), [forecast-plugin move discussion](https://groups.google.com/g/beancount/c/LNDGdrCNa54/m/oCY0oSKVAQAJ)

**What it does.** The experimental plugin expands annotated templates for weekly/monthly/yearly forecasts with controls such as until, repeat, and skip. Beanlabs explicitly offers no stability guarantee.

**Adopt.** Keep generated entries scenario-only and bounded by the report horizon.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not depend on an experimental plugin, copy its annotation DSL, or create synthetic booked transactions.

### 15. Maybe Finance — an instructive unshipped roadmap

**Sources:** [archived repository](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe), [roadmap](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/wiki/Roadmap)

**What it does.** Maybe listed recurring/future transactions as planned and forecasting as a consideration, but the repository was archived in July 2025 before that roadmap became reliable implementation evidence.

**Adopt.** Maintenance state matters when evaluating open-source precedent; distinguish a roadmap from shipped behavior.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not cite unimplemented plans as validation or build against an archived codebase.

### 16. Sure (`we-promise/sure`) discussion #3033 — proposed bills subsystem

**Source:** [Bills subsystem proposal, discussion #3033](https://github.com/we-promise/sure/discussions/3033)

**What it does.** Sure is the active community successor to Maybe. The August 2026 proposal adds recurrence rules, individual bill occurrences, allocations to payments, paid/partial/overdue state, explainable matching, calendars, subscriptions, and paycheck planning. Maintainers welcomed a staged contribution and the author opened PR #3083, but the discussion does not establish that the subsystem is merged.

**Adopt.** Its decomposition is the clearest evidence for the Cash Lens leaf boundary: declaration and schedule first; occurrence/payment allocation and matching later. Preserve a stable obligation ID so 3.5.2 can reference it.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not import its occurrence table, allocation model, fuzzy matcher, AI tools, calendar feed, paycheck planner, or partial-payment states. They are a much larger surface and most map directly to 3.5.2 or later leaves.

### 17. Plaid recurring transactions — history-derived predictions

**Source:** [Plaid Transactions API (`/transactions/recurring/get`)](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/)

**What it does.** Plaid returns detected recurring streams with fields including `predicted_next_date`, `is_active`, and lifecycle states such as tombstoned. Updates arrive after provider transaction history exists.

**Adopt.** Continue treating detected streams as predictions that can go stale; source labels should explain why they exist.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** A provider prediction cannot represent rent or tuition before any bank sees it. Do not require another Plaid product or provider scope for a user declaration.

### 18. RFC 5545 — iCalendar recurrence rules

**Source:** [RFC 5545](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5545)

**What it does.** `RRULE` can encode broad calendar recurrence. Invalid generated dates are ignored and are not counted, so a monthly day-31 rule omits shorter months rather than clamping to their final day.

**Adopt.** Be explicit about recurrence edge semantics and preserve an anchor independently from each clamped occurrence.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not store opaque RRULE strings. Their grammar, invalid-date behavior, timezone concepts, and interoperability burden exceed five fixed cadences and conflict with Cash Lens’s existing month-end-clamped behavior.

### 19. RFC 7529 — alternate scales and invalid-date skipping

**Source:** [RFC 7529](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7529)

**What it does.** It extends recurrence with `RSCALE` and `SKIP=OMIT|BACKWARD|FORWARD`; it also recommends explicit last-day forms such as `BYMONTHDAY=-1` when that is the intent.

**Adopt.** Name Cash Lens’s policy directly: preserve the original numerical anchor and clamp backward to month end.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Calendar scales, forward skipping, and generalized rule parsing solve interoperability problems this leaf does not have.

### 20. `rrule` — mature iCalendar recurrence library

**Sources:** [npm package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/rrule), [GitHub repository](https://github.com/jkbrzt/rrule)

**What it does.** `rrule` is an established TypeScript implementation of iCalendar recurrence. The latest npm release observed during research was 2.8.1 from November 2023, and upstream activity appears limited relative to the extra parser/timezone surface it would introduce.

**Adopt.** Nothing is needed from the dependency for `once | weekly | biweekly | monthly | annual`; use its existence as a threshold test—choose it only if Cash Lens later needs interoperable RRULEs.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** Do not add a dependency whose larger semantics differ from the existing projector and whose maintenance posture would become part of a financial-data attack surface.

### 21. `rrule-temporal` — modern generalized alternative

**Source:** [`rrule-temporal` repository](https://github.com/ggaabe/rrule-temporal)

**What it does.** This newer Temporal-based project targets RFC 5545 recurrence, timezone handling, and RFC 7529 behavior with a more modern date foundation.

**Adopt.** Re-evaluate a maintained Temporal-based engine if a later leaf requires timezones, arbitrary recurrence, or calendar interchange.

**Reject for 3.5.1.** It is still substantially broader than date-only fixed cadences and would add a dependency without reducing Cash Lens’s current implementation or security burden.

## Cross-source conclusions

1. **Expectation is not ledger truth.** Ledger CLI, Firefly III, Lunch Money, and the duplicate failures in Actual all support keeping declarations and projected occurrences separate from booked transactions.
2. **A small explicit model wins.** Buxfer, YNAB, Quicken, and GnuCash converge on name, account, amount, first date, and recurrence; Cash Lens needs no generalized rules language.
3. **Use the surface that already exists.** Monarch, Rocket Money, Copilot, and Tiller demonstrate the value of list plus calendar. Cash Lens already has both under `/upcoming`.
4. **Do not guess identity.** Actual’s duplicate history and Sure’s much larger matching design show that “same-looking” is not “same transaction.” Any automatic settlement or suppression belongs behind the 3.5.2 evidence model.
5. **Keep detected and declared lifecycles different.** A detected stream may become stale when history stops. An explicit obligation remains user intent until its declared end or the user ends it.
6. **Preserve Cash Lens date semantics.** Monthly/annual schedules keep the original day as the anchor and clamp to month end, rather than RFC 5545/Actual omission semantics.
7. **No new provider or dependency is justified.** This leaf needs no token, scope, webhook, upload, worker, queue, or recurrence package.

## Recommended prior-art synthesis

Store one user-owned `scheduled_obligations` declaration per known charge. Keep it separate from `transactions` and history-derived `recurring_streams`. Support one-time, weekly, biweekly, monthly, and annual schedules with an optional inclusive end date. Bind each declaration to an account, store a positive minor-unit amount and currency, and project negative outflow occurrences on read through the existing Upcoming reference-date/month-end seam. Preserve source identity in the UI. End rather than generate/delete ledger events. Defer matching, payment state, variance, partial payment, and alerts to 3.5.2.

The founder approved the safety-first policy: show both sources with `Known`/`Predicted` labels and a non-destructive possible-overlap warning, count both, and never hide money using an identity guess.
