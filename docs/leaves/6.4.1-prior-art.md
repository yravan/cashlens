# 6.4.1 Recurring charge detection — prior art

Survey for the detector (grouping, cadence, amount tolerance, thresholds) and the
confirm/dismiss lifecycle. Sources numbered; adopted/rejected decisions at the end.

## Provider prior art — the output contract

1. **Plaid Recurring Transactions** (`/transactions/recurring/get`;
   plaid.com/docs/transactions/, plaid.com/blog/recurring-transactions/,
   plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/). Groups history into inflow and
   outflow *streams* "based on the description, amount, and cadence". A stream is
   **mature at ≥3 occurrences**; below that it's early-detection only. Frequency
   enum: `WEEKLY / BIWEEKLY / SEMI_MONTHLY / MONTHLY / ANNUALLY / UNKNOWN`. Streams
   carry `average_amount`, `last_amount`, `last_date`, `predicted_next_date`,
   `is_active`, `status`. Plaid recommends **≥180 days of history**. Model is ML;
   the *shape* of the output (merchant group + cadence class + typical amount +
   last seen + predicted next) is the industry contract, and the one 6.4.2 needs.

## Open-source implementations

2. **Actual Budget — "Find schedules"**
   (`packages/loot-core/src/server/schedules/find-schedules.ts`). Rule-based:
   candidate cadence configs (weekly, every-2-weeks, monthly-by-day, monthly-last-day,
   1st/3rd + 2nd/4th weekday-of-month) slid one day at a time over scan windows; each
   candidate generates **exactly 3 expected occurrences** and requires an identical-payee
   transaction within **±2 days** of each, amount within `getApproxNumberThreshold`
   (~7.5%). Rank `sum(1/(dayDiff+1))`, grouped by payee, best rank wins. Monthly start
   days > 28 skipped ("28 is the max number of days all months are guaranteed to
   have"). Winner becomes a schedule with `is`/`isapprox` date and amount conditions.
3. **Firefly III** (docs.firefly-iii.org recurring + subscriptions): no auto-detection —
   user-defined templates (Google-Calendar-style repetition). Useful convention: a
   transaction repeating on the 29th/30th/31st **falls back to the previous day**
   when the month is short.
4. **Maybe Finance** (github.com/maybe-finance/maybe wiki/Roadmap, PR #883): recurring
   detection is roadmap-only; shipped work is a rules engine and transfer matching.
   No detector to borrow.
5. **YNAB** (api.ynab.com scheduled transactions; toolkit-for-ynab issues): scheduled
   transactions are **user-created only** with a fixed frequency enum (weekly,
   everyOtherWeek, twiceAMonth, monthly, yearly, …). No detection — confirms the
   fixed-cadence-class vocabulary, not the algorithm.

## Commercial apps — detection behavior and confirm/dismiss UX

6. **Lunch Money** (support.lunchmoney.app recurring-items; adamtaylor.me post):
   detects when "a clear pattern of payee and amount repeat over a regular cadence" —
   concretely **the same payment 2 months in a row** for monthly items. Detections are
   *suggested*, on a separate list, and must be **approved or rejected**. Approval
   creates a matching rule: payee + amount + a **few-day window around the expected
   date** (e.g. billed ~3rd → match 1st–5th), which prevents same-amount false matches
   elsewhere in the month.
7. **Monarch Money** (help.monarch.com Tracking-Recurring-Expenses-and-Bills;
   codafinancialcoaching.com): every sync re-scans for new recurring items; a
   **Recurring Review flow** presents candidates for approval, edit (amount,
   frequency, date), or "not recurring". Third-party estimate: ~80% of bills caught,
   missing when amount or billing date varies. "Mark as not recurring" lives on the
   merchant, and is reversible via merchant settings — dismissal is **merchant-level
   negative memory**.
8. **Copilot Money** (help.copilot.money Recurrings collection): recurrings are
   created *from a transaction*; Copilot **estimates the frequency and the user
   confirms it**. Ongoing matching is filter-based (name substring + amount range +
   date window); "Not Recurring" on a transaction removes it from a series. Docs
   warn that names vary month to month — filters should target the stable substring
   ("Uber" not "Uber One").
9. **Rocket Money** (help.rocketmoney.com; rocketmoney.com/feature/manage-subscriptions):
   fully automatic detection over Plaid data into a Recurring tab, with an
   **Inactive** section for streams that stopped; alerts on price increases. Manual
   add works by selecting the transactions that belong to the subscription.
10. **Quicken Simplifi** (support.simplifi.quicken.com Managing-Recurring-Transactions;
    info.quicken.com Subscriptions tab): auto-creates recurring *series* after
    connect; desktop scans **14 months** of history (enough to catch annuals);
    reminders match new transactions by payee + amount (exact or range — user-tunable
    when mismatches happen) + expected date; unmatched reminders surface for manual
    linking, which improves future matching.

## Algorithms — literature and patents

11. **Ntropy engineering blog** ("Quickly finding recurring events in massive
    sequences", ntropy.com): dates as integers; find longest subsequences with a
    **constant interval ≤ K=31 days** via a hashmap scan (O(N·K)); no skipped steps
    allowed in the idealized version. Explicitly flags the real world — varying month
    lengths, late payments — and says production uses fuzzy matching on
    transaction-to-transaction distance. Lesson: exact-interval subsequence mining is
    clean but brittle; tolerate day-level jitter and classify into named cadences.
12. **Subaio** (subaio.com, vendor explainer, page now partially dead): bank-deployed
    recurring detection; required fields are just **merchant name, amount, currency**;
    output separates recurring payments / subscriptions / one-offs.
13. **ACM ICAIF '24, "Unveiling Recurring Financial Patterns"**
    (dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3677052.3698596): unsupervised filtering — group into
    candidate series, keep series whose **amount σ is small and whose gaps are stable**
    (drop any series with a gap > median×1.1). Median-based interval stability beats
    mean-based under one late payment.
14. **US 5,918,216** (Intuit, "Automatic recognition of periods for financial
    transactions"): classic formulation — compare each inter-transaction gap against
    candidate period lengths; recurring when |gap − period| ≤ tolerance for all gaps.
15. **US 11,144,935** (interval statistics): uses average gap + gap standard deviation
    (and their ratio) as the recurrence features.
16. **US 7,693,771** (identifying recurring payments): same-interval detection between
    payments, amount similarity secondary.
17. **US 8,380,590** (detecting recurring *income*): paychecks are the inflow case —
    same-employer deposits at weekly/biweekly/semi-monthly/monthly periods with pay
    jitter; direction-agnostic detection is standard.

## Merchant/description normalization

18. **Brex engineering** (medium.com/brexeng, merchant classification): strip
    processor prefixes (`SQ *`, `TST*`, `PAYPAL *`, …) before any grouping; the
    residue is the merchant key.
19. **FinGoal** (kristastadler.medium.com) + descriptor-anatomy sources
    (paylosophy.com, solidgate.com glossary, context.dev): descriptors are
    ~22 chars: processor prefix + brand + trailing store numbers (`#4821`),
    city/state, and **random order-reference suffixes** (`AMZN Mktp US*RT4KZ8`) that
    change per charge — the main enemy of naive string grouping. Visa/Mastercard
    patents (US 11,989,165, US 11,023,889) formalize staged stripping: remove
    transaction-reference tail, then location tokens, then normalize.

## Decisions

**Adopted**
- **Output contract = Plaid's stream shape** (1): merchant + direction + cadence
  class + typical amount + last date, with `next expected` derivable — exactly what
  6.4.2 consumes.
- **Cadence classes, not free intervals** (1, 5, 11): weekly / biweekly /
  semimonthly / monthly / annual buckets with per-class day tolerance beats raw
  interval clustering — month lengths vary, payments post late.
- **Median interval → class, per-gap tolerance check** (13, 14): median gap picks
  the class; every gap must sit within the class's day tolerance, so one wild gap
  disqualifies (14) but one *late* payment inside tolerance doesn't (13).
- **≥3 occurrences** (1, 2): Plaid's maturity bar and Actual's hard requirement.
  Lunch Money's 2-in-a-row (6) ships too many false positives for an auto-list;
  with confirmation UX, 3 is the sweet spot.
- **Amount tolerance ~ Actual's approx threshold** (2): typical amount = median;
  each occurrence within a relative band (utilities drift, subscriptions don't).
  Exact-amount groups additionally merge across small drifts via the merchant key.
- **Suggest → confirm/dismiss** (6, 7): detections are proposals; user intent
  (confirmed/dismissed) is the only persisted state, keyed on the stream identity —
  the transfer_pairs dismissal-memory pattern, at stream level like Monarch's
  merchant-level "not recurring" (7).
- **Both directions** (1, 17): inflow streams (paychecks) and outflow streams.
- **Normalization before grouping** (18, 19): uppercase, strip `*`-reference tails,
  trailing `#123`/digit runs, collapse whitespace; prefer the enriched `merchant`
  field when present (Plaid already normalizes it), fall back to cleaned
  description.
- **Posted-only, transfer-pair exclusion** (repo law): active-pair members are
  internal moves, not charges; pending rows are unstable.

**Rejected**
- **ML/embedding grouping** (1, 12): non-deterministic, unexplainable, untestable to
  exact values — against the repo's testing policy; deterministic rules are the
  transfer-matching precedent.
- **Sliding candidate start-date search** (2): Actual slides day-by-day because it
  synthesizes *schedules*; we only classify existing history, so gaps between
  actual dates suffice — far less code, same answer.
- **Exact-interval subsequence mining** (11): brittle under real posting jitter;
  Ntropy themselves abandon it in production.
- **2-occurrence detection** (6) and **early-detection streams** (1): deferred;
  false-positive cost lands on the confirm list. 6.4.3's manager can add them.
- **Auto-rules from confirmations** (6, 8): matching future transactions to
  confirmed streams is 6.4.2/6.4.3 territory; detection here is recomputed from
  history every time, so new occurrences attach by construction.
- **Day-of-month anchoring (1st/3rd weekday patterns)** (2): US-bill nicety, small
  audience, large case surface; monthly-by-day tolerance covers it.
