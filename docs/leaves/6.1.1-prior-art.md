# 6.1.1 Cash-flow summary (true spend) — prior-art survey

The leaf: dashboard of money in, money out, and net by month — with true spend: transfers and
card payments never double-count. Survey question: how do production personal-finance systems
present monthly cash flow, and what do they include in it?

## Sources

Commercial products

1. Monarch Money — [Cash Flow](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20504904768020-Cash-Flow):
   summary card = total income, total spending, net cash flow, savings rate for the viewed
   period; charts group by month; every number click-throughs to the transactions behind it.
2. Monarch Money — [Transfers and Credit Card Payments](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393292-Transfers-and-Credit-Card-Payments):
   transfers "aren't new spending — they're just shifting money you already have," so the
   Transfers category group is excluded from budget, cash flow, and spending totals; the card
   purchase counts when swiped, the later card payment never counts again.
3. Evolving Money Coaching — [Monarch transfer pitfalls](https://evolvingmoneycoaching.com/avoid-these-mistakes-in-monarch-money-and-an-explanation-on-transfers/):
   the failure mode of category-driven exclusion — Zelle/Venmo payments auto-categorized as
   "Transfers" silently vanish from every report, and true transfers miscategorized as spending
   double-count. Exclusion hangs off a user-editable label, so one wrong category corrupts flow.
4. Quicken Simplifi — [Handling Transfers in the Spending Plan](https://support.simplifi.quicken.com/en/articles/5142302-handling-transfers-in-the-spending-plan):
   transfers including credit-card payments are "neither income nor expenses" and excluded by
   default; excluded rows stay visible in activity with an "Excluded" marker (visible exclusion,
   not deletion).
5. Quicken Simplifi — [Exclude/include from reports](https://support.simplifi.quicken.com/en/articles/3569626-how-to-exclude-and-include-transactions-from-reports-and-the-spending-plan):
   transfer-category rows are always excluded from Reports, even when force-included in the
   Spending Plan; per-side include toggles exist for linked transfers.
6. Empower Personal Dashboard — [Why credit card payments are not in Cash Flow](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/201170070-Why-are-my-Credit-Card-payments-not-included-in-the-Cash-Flow-section):
   payments are transfers between your own accounts; the linked card's transactions are the
   expenses, "including the payment as well would result in double-counting."
7. Empower Personal Dashboard — [Cash Flow graph](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/201169700-What-is-the-Cash-Flow-Graph-and-How-is-it-Useful):
   income and expenses month over month, defaulting to the current month vs previous; transfers,
   card payments, and securities trades excluded.
8. Lunch Money — [Category properties](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/category-properties):
   "Exclude from totals" on the Payment/Transfer category removes rows from income and spending
   alike; also documents refusing to zero cross-currency transfer groups (rate mismatch) — the
   same reason Cash Lens leaves cross-currency transfers unmatched.
9. Copilot Money — [Cash Flow tab](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/9682232-cash-flow-tab-overview):
   net income = income minus spend per month; excluded spend is out of totals by default with an
   explicit "show excluded spend" toggle — exclusion is disclosed, not silent.
10. YNAB — [Pending Transactions](https://www.ynab.com/blog/pending-transactions-have-arrived):
    pending rows are a read-only preview that doesn't count until cleared; amounts routinely
    change between authorization and post (the restaurant-tip case), and the posted version can
    arrive as a distinct entry.
11. Mint (canonical anti-example) — [White Coat Investor thread](https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/forum/personal-finance-and-budgeting/3255-mint-double-counting-expenses),
    [Intuit community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/credit-cards/discussion/mint-bulk-tagging-credit-card-purchases-counted-twice/00/2748636),
    [abacuswealth workarounds](https://abacuswealth.com/five-mint-problems-and-their-solutions/):
    card payments counted as expenses on top of the card's own transactions roughly doubled
    reported spending; the user-side fix was hand-categorizing payments as transfers, forever.
    This is the exact failure 3.3.1 + 6.1.1 exist to make structurally impossible.

Open source

12. Firefly III — [issue #312](https://github.com/firefly-iii/firefly-iii/issues/312) and
    [transaction docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/explanation/financial-concepts/transactions/):
    transfer is a first-class transaction type between own asset accounts, ignored by
    expense/revenue reports because "no true revenue or expense has been realized"; mistyped
    imports (transfer stored as withdrawal+deposit) are the recurring corruption bug.
13. Firefly III — [multi-currency discussion #5639](https://github.com/orgs/firefly-iii/discussions/5639):
    no automatic conversion; "the strategy is to split multiple currencies as much as possible:
    double bars, double charts, double report entries… any time you see money being mixed up,
    that's the bug."
14. Maybe Finance — [issue #858 / PR #883](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/883),
    [Budgeting V1 PR #1584](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/1584),
    [matching PR #1585](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/1585),
    [v0.6.0 notes](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/releases/tag/v0.6.0):
    transfers are first-class two-way records auto-detected by amount+account+date proximity —
    they cannot even be categorized — and are excluded from income/expense/budget totals; the
    earlier one-sided `marked_as_transfer` boolean was ripped out as unmaintainable. Loan
    payments are the named exception they keep in budgets.
15. Actual Budget — [Reports docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/reports/): cash flow = income
    and expense over budgeted accounts; transfers are linked two-sided entries, which is what
    keeps them out of income/expense math.
16. Plaid — [Transactions API](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/) and
    [transaction states](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/): `date` is a
    bare ISO calendar date with no timezone, institution-reported; pending rows post 1–5+ days
    later as a *new* transaction id (the pending id lands in `removed`), and amounts may change
    at post time.
17. Quicken classic — [Income vs Expenses over time](https://community.quicken.com/discussion/7929872/new-income-vs-expense-bar-chart):
    month-interval income/expense columns with per-month drill-down into categories — the
    decades-old convention this dashboard's table mirrors.

## What everyone agrees on

- Cash flow is income vs spending vs net, bucketed by calendar month, drillable to rows.
- Internal transfers and credit-card payments are not flow. Every surveyed system excludes them;
  the one that didn't reliably (Mint) is the cautionary tale users left over.
- Exclusion is disclosed, not silent: Simplifi's "Excluded" marker, Copilot's toggle, Monarch's
  help copy. Rows never disappear from the ledger view itself.

## Adopted

- **Exclusion by pair membership, not category** (Maybe's model, the repo's 3.3.1 contract): a
  transaction in an active `transfer_pair` is out of flow whatever its category says. Monarch,
  Lunch Money, and Mint all hang exclusion off a category label, and sources 3 and 11 document
  how that corrupts reports in both directions. Deterministic zero-sum pairing is the wedge.
- **Calendar-month buckets from the date-only column.** `transactions.date` is a Postgres `date`
  (Plaid's own `date` is timezone-free), so the bucket is a pure string prefix — no clock read,
  no timezone math, deterministic under test. Month labels are rendered from the string, never
  through a `Date` (UTC-midnight parsing shifts days in negative-offset zones).
- **Per-currency separation, no FX** (Firefly's rule, repo law since 6.3.1): one section per
  currency; unlike currencies never combined into one number.
- **Posted-only totals, disclosed.** YNAB's evidence: pending rows are previews whose amounts
  change and whose posted versions arrive as new rows (Plaid confirms: new id at post). Locally
  decisive: the 3.3.1 matcher pairs only posted rows, so an included pending transfer leg could
  never be paired and would double-count — the exact bug this leaf exists to prevent; and
  pending-to-posted reconciliation (3.2.1) hasn't landed, so a pending row and its posted
  version can coexist for non-Plaid sources. The dashboard states the rule and shows the live
  pending count instead of hiding it (Copilot/Simplifi disclosure pattern).
- **Visible exclusion count** (Simplifi's "Excluded this month" pattern): the dashboard says how
  many rows are treated as internal transfers, so true spend is auditable at a glance.
- **Month rows link into the ledger** (Monarch/Quicken drill-through) via the existing 3.6.1 URL
  schema (`from`/`to`/`currency`) — reuse, not new query surface; the real drill-down view is
  6.1.2.

## Rejected

- **Category-driven exclusion** (Monarch/Lunch Money/Mint) — the failure mode is the product
  thesis; see sources 3 and 11.
- **FX conversion to a base currency** (Lunch Money) — needs a rate source and re-rate policy;
  repo has none by design (3.3.1 leaves cross-currency transfers unmatched for the same reason).
- **Counting pending with reconciliation caveats** (Monarch) — unsafe here until 3.2.1; the
  matcher's posted-only domain makes pending inclusion actively wrong for transfer legs.
- **Savings rate / Sankey / month-over-month deltas** (Monarch, Copilot) — insight layers on top
  of the same aggregates; nothing in the leaf's promise, natural 6.1.2/6.1.3 territory.
- **Configurable inclusion toggles** (Copilot's excluded-spend switch, Simplifi's per-side
  include) — settings surface without a settings page (1.2.2); the fixed rule plus disclosure
  covers the MVP.
- **Zero-filled month ranges / current-month framing** (Empower defaults to "this month") —
  needs a clock; the repo's determinism rule (nothing under test reads the clock) is satisfied
  by deriving visible months from the data itself.
