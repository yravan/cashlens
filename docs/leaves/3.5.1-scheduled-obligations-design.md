# Leaf 3.5.1 — Scheduled obligations: approved design

Status: **Approved by the founder on 2026-09-14**

Research basis: [`3.5.1-prior-art.md`](./3.5.1-prior-art.md)

## Founder rulings

1. Every scheduled obligation requires an account.
2. An exact known-versus-predicted overlap shows both sources, warns that both are counted, and keeps both in totals.
3. The recommended design below is approved for implementation.

## Decision summary

Add a separate, user-owned `scheduled_obligations` table for explicit future charges. A row is a declaration, not a transaction and not a detected recurring stream. Project virtual occurrences on read into the existing `/upcoming` calendar, lists, and per-currency totals.

Approved product boundary:

- Outflows only: the leaf says “charges” and names rent, insurance, and tuition.
- One-time, weekly, biweekly, monthly, and annual schedules.
- Required account assignment.
- Positive stored amount; convert it to the ledger’s negative outflow convention in Upcoming.
- Optional inclusive end date for repeating schedules.
- Add, edit, and end; no hard delete or restore in the feature UI.
- Keep known declarations, detected predictions, and actual transactions separate.
- If a known occurrence exactly overlaps a detected prediction, show both, label both, warn, and count both until 3.5.2 can establish identity.
- No new library, provider scope, secret, webhook, upload, queue, or background job.

## Exact scope

`FEATURES.md:112` promises:

> **3.5.1 Scheduled obligations** `[P1]` — Record known future charges — rent, insurance, tuition — so they appear in upcoming views before any bank sees them.

This design satisfies that promise by letting a signed-in person declare a charge and immediately see its virtual occurrences in the Upcoming month, even when no provider transaction exists.

### Explicitly outside this leaf

`3.5.2 Expectation matching` owns all of the following:

- Linking an arrived transaction to an obligation.
- Deciding that an expected occurrence was paid, partially paid, missed, or duplicated.
- Automatically removing or suppressing an occurrence because a transaction looks similar.
- Amount/date tolerance and wrong-amount detection.
- Overdue alerts and notifications.
- Payment allocation and split/partial-payment state.
- Repairing historical duplicates.

Also deferred: expected income, scheduled transfers, balance forecasting, categories, arbitrary recurrence, provider bill-pay/cancellation, and reminders.

## Existing Cash Lens seams to preserve

- `transactions` are ledger facts. A projection must never become one.
- `recurring_streams` stores sparse decisions attached to patterns recomputed from posted history. It is not a declaration store.
- `projectUpcoming(..., reference)` is pure and projects through the reference month’s end.
- The page’s existing `?on=YYYY-MM-DD` parameter is the testable clock seam.
- Weekly and biweekly cadence use UTC date arithmetic; monthly and annual cadence preserve the original anchor day and clamp to the target month’s final day.
- Detected proposed and confirmed streams project; dismissed and canceled streams do not.
- Detected streams can become stale after two missed cycles.
- Upcoming totals remain separate by currency and by inflow/outflow; unlike currencies are never combined.
- User data is read and written only through `lib/data`, with identity derived from Clerk and every query inside `withRequestScope`.
- API mutation precedence remains authentication, origin, closed parsing, then resource identity.

## Approaches considered

### A. Materialize future transactions

Create future ledger rows now and later merge an imported bank transaction into them.

**Why it is proven:** YNAB, Actual, GnuCash, and spreadsheet workflows use variants of scheduled/generated transactions.

**Why it is rejected:** Actual’s duplicate reports and later merge tooling demonstrate the failure mode. Correctness would depend on the matching system explicitly assigned to 3.5.2. It would also let forecasts contaminate transaction history, cash flow, categorization, and balances.

### B. Put declarations in `recurring_streams`

Add a manual mode or nullable history fields to the existing detected-stream table.

**Why it is rejected:** The table’s identity is account/currency/direction/normalized history name/cadence, and its lifecycle follows recomputed transaction evidence. A declared tuition payment may be one-time and have no history. Mixing the concepts would spread nullable state and make detected staleness incorrectly apply to user intent.

### C. Separate declaration rows with read-time projection — recommended

Persist only the user’s schedule definition and expand virtual occurrences for Upcoming.

**Why it fits:** Ledger CLI, Firefly III’s conceptual split, Lunch Money’s occurrence view, and Sure’s proposed declaration/occurrence separation all support it. It preserves ledger integrity, makes ownership enforceable, and leaves a stable obligation ID for 3.5.2 without prebuilding that leaf.

### D. Store RFC 5545 RRULEs or add a recurrence dependency

**Why it is rejected:** Cash Lens needs five fixed date-only choices. RRULE brings a broad grammar, invalid-date omission, timezones, and dependency risk; its day-31 behavior conflicts with Cash Lens’s existing clamp rule. Both `rrule` and `rrule-temporal` are substantially larger than this need.

## Recommended data model

### New enum

`scheduled_obligation_cadence`:

- `once`
- `weekly`
- `biweekly`
- `monthly`
- `annual`

Use a separate database enum rather than adding `once` to the detected-stream cadence enum. A one-time declaration is valid here but not a recurring detector result.

### New table: `scheduled_obligations`

| Column | Type | Null | Meaning |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `id` | `uuid` | no | Stable declaration identity; random default |
| `user_id` | `uuid` | no | Owner; FK to `users`, cascade on user purge |
| `account_id` | `uuid` | no | Account expected to carry the charge |
| `name` | `text` | no | User-facing label, trimmed, 1–200 characters |
| `amount_minor` | `bigint` number mode | no | Positive, nonzero magnitude in the declared currency |
| `currency` | `char(3)` | no | Uppercase three-letter currency code |
| `cadence` | enum above | no | One of the five supported schedule choices |
| `starts_on` | PostgreSQL `date` | no | The one-time date or first repeating occurrence |
| `ends_on` | PostgreSQL `date` | yes | Inclusive final occurrence date for a repeating schedule |
| `ended_at` | timezone-aware timestamp | yes | User ended the declaration; ended rows do not project |
| `created_at` | timezone-aware timestamp | no | Database default `now()` |
| `updated_at` | timezone-aware timestamp | no | Database default `now()`, advanced by edits/end |

### Database constraints

- Primary key on `id`.
- Unique `(id, user_id)` to permit future same-owner composite references from 3.5.2.
- Composite FK `(account_id, user_id) → accounts(id, user_id)` with cascade on account purge. This prevents a cross-user account reference even beneath forced RLS.
- `amount_minor > 0`.
- `amount_minor <= 9007199254740991` (`Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`).
- `currency ~ '^[A-Z]{3}$'`.
- `name = btrim(name)` and `char_length(name) BETWEEN 1 AND 200`.
- `ends_on IS NULL OR ends_on >= starts_on`.
- `cadence <> 'once' OR ends_on IS NULL`.
- No uniqueness constraint over account/name/date/amount. Two real obligations may be identical on every visible field.
- Partial active-list index beginning with `user_id` and `starts_on`, restricted to `ended_at IS NULL`; index the composite account reference if the generated schema does not already do so.

### Deliberately absent fields

- No `transaction_id`, `paid_at`, occurrence table, or match status: 3.5.2.
- No `direction`: every row is a charge/outflow.
- No signed amount: positive magnitude makes the input and CHECK unambiguous.
- No `category_id`: categorization is not needed to appear in Upcoming.
- No merchant, notes, provider ID, confidence, or recurrence-rule string.
- No user timezone or time-of-day: these are date-only obligations.

## Lifecycle and invariants

1. **Create** stores one active declaration (`ended_at IS NULL`). A start date in the past is valid; the parser does not read the clock.
2. **Edit** is a full-state replacement of editable schedule fields. It can change account, name, amount/currency, cadence, start, and end date while the row is active.
3. **End** sets `ended_at` once and retains the row. A retry is idempotent and preserves the original `ended_at`.
4. Ended rows never project or appear in the active management list, regardless of the `?on` reference.
5. An edit may not revive an ended row. If end wins a race, a later edit sees the same 404 as any unavailable obligation.
6. Account purge cascades the declaration because an obligation cannot remain actionable without its required account. Existing purge confirmation copy must disclose that known obligations attached to the account are also removed.
7. A finite schedule reaching `ends_on` does not prove its final charge was paid. Its latest valid occurrence may remain as one passed-date item until the person ends it or 3.5.2 settles it.

## Security, RLS, and grants

### Request-scoped access

- All DAL functions live under `apps/web/lib/data`.
- They call `requireUser()`/`auth()` and derive the owner; no route or caller supplies `user_id`.
- Every read/write runs inside `withRequestScope(user.clerkUserId, ...)` as `cashlens_app`.
- Queries include explicit own-user predicates in addition to RLS.
- Account and obligation identifiers are untrusted opaque strings until the boundary and DAL validate them.
- SQL is composed through Drizzle; no raw input is interpolated into SQL.

### Policies

Enable and force row-level security. For `cashlens_app`, define:

- `SELECT`: `USING (user_id = (select app_current_user_id()))`.
- `INSERT`: `WITH CHECK (user_id = (select app_current_user_id()))`.
- `UPDATE`: both `USING` and `WITH CHECK` use the same own-row expression.

No policy or grant permits direct DELETE. User/account purge can still cascade through owner-controlled parent deletion.

### Least-privilege grants

Grant only:

- `SELECT` on the table.
- Column-scoped `INSERT` for `user_id`, `account_id`, `name`, `amount_minor`, `currency`, `cadence`, `starts_on`, and `ends_on`; IDs/timestamps use database defaults.
- Column-scoped `UPDATE` for `account_id`, `name`, `amount_minor`, `currency`, `cadence`, `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `ended_at`, and `updated_at`.

Do not grant UPDATE on `id`, `user_id`, or `created_at`; do not grant DELETE.

### Required isolation evidence

API tests must prove that user B cannot list, edit, end, or infer user A’s obligation, and user A cannot bind an obligation to user B’s account. A raw runtime-role query must independently prove forced RLS. Grant probes must prove forbidden owner/identity-column updates and DELETE fail with insufficient privilege.

### New risk surface declaration

This leaf adds one user-owned financial-intent table and three same-origin mutations. It adds **no** provider scope, secret, token, upload, webhook, background job, public endpoint, or dependency. The mitigation for the new data surface is forced RLS, composite ownership, closed input, narrow grants, and dual-user tests.

## API contracts

All mutations are same-origin POST route handlers behind `guardPost`.

### `POST /api/obligations`

Creates a declaration.

Request body, with exactly these keys:

```json
{
  "accountId": "00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000000",
  "name": "Rent",
  "amount": "1800.00",
  "currency": "USD",
  "cadence": "monthly",
  "startsOn": "2026-04-05",
  "endsOn": null
}
```

Success: `201 { "obligationId": "00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001" }` (illustrative UUID).

### `POST /api/obligations/[obligationId]`

Full-state edit of an active declaration. It accepts the same exact body as create.

Success: `200 {}`.

### `POST /api/obligations/[obligationId]/end`

Ends an active declaration. The body must be exactly `{}`. Repeating the operation for the same own row is a successful no-op and does not replace its original end timestamp.

Success: `200 {}`.

### Closed parsing rules

- The JSON root must be a plain object, not null or an array.
- Unknown, missing, or incorrectly typed fields return the same `400 { "error": "invalid_request" }`.
- `name` is trimmed once; the result must be 1–200 characters.
- `amount` is a string in the existing `parseMajorUnits` grammar: unsigned plain decimal only, no sign, exponent, grouping separator, or excess fractional precision. Its parsed minor value must be from 1 through `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`.
- `currency` must match `^[A-Z]{3}$`; do not silently invent or combine a currency. The UI defaults it from the chosen account but it remains explicit and may differ.
- `cadence` must be one of the five values.
- `startsOn` and non-null `endsOn` must pass the shared `isIsoDate` real-calendar-date check.
- `endsOn`, when present, must be on or after `startsOn`.
- `once` requires `endsOn: null`.
- `accountId` must be a string at structural parse time. UUID shape and ownership are resource-identity checks and therefore return 404, not a distinguishable validation error.
- `obligationId` in the route follows the same malformed/unknown/cross-user 404 rule.
- Error responses contain no submitted values, database detail, or IDs.

### Exact error precedence

The complete precedence is below; identity steps apply only when the route uses that resource:

1. No authenticated Clerk session → `401 { "error": "unauthorized" }`.
2. Authenticated cross-origin browser request → `403 { "error": "cross_origin" }`.
3. Malformed JSON or a body that violates the closed schema/value rules → `400 { "error": "invalid_request" }`.
4. Malformed, unknown, ended-when-editing, or cross-user obligation identity → byte-identical `404 { "error": "obligation_not_found" }`.
5. Malformed, unknown, or cross-user account identity → byte-identical `404 { "error": "account_not_found" }`.
6. A successful create/edit/end returns the route-specific success above.

For edit, obligation identity is resolved before account identity after parsing. A concurrent account purge that wins between validation and mutation is mapped to the same `account_not_found` response rather than exposing a database error. Unexpected failures return a sanitized generic server error and are not logged with names, amounts, or body payloads.

## Recurrence and projection rules

### Date semantics

All schedule fields are PostgreSQL `date` values. Recurrence uses UTC calendar arithmetic only; no timestamp or locale conversion participates.

- `once`: emit only `starts_on`.
- `weekly`: add 7 calendar days from `starts_on`.
- `biweekly`: add 14 calendar days from `starts_on`.
- `monthly`: preserve the original day from `starts_on`; clamp to the target month’s last day.
- `annual`: preserve the original month and day; clamp February 29 to February 28 in non-leap years and return to February 29 in leap years.
- `ends_on` is inclusive: an occurrence equal to it exists; a later one does not.
- A date equal to `reference` is upcoming, not passed.

The original start day remains the anchor. Advancing from a clamped result must not produce January 31 → February 28 → March 28 drift.

### Bounded projection

The pure obligation projector takes declarations and an explicit `reference`; it never calls the clock.

For each non-ended declaration:

1. Find the latest valid occurrence strictly before `reference`, if one exists, and emit at most that one with `overdue: true`.
2. Emit each valid occurrence from `reference` through `monthEndOf(reference)`, inclusive.
3. Apply `starts_on` and inclusive `ends_on` to every candidate.
4. Do not mark an explicit declaration stale because bank history went quiet.

The projector must jump to the occurrence around the reference using day/month/year arithmetic. It must not loop from an arbitrarily old `starts_on`; runtime is bounded by occurrences in the visible month, not schedule age.

A passed one-time or final finite occurrence remains visible until the declaration is ended or future matching settles it. Its UI copy is **“Scheduled date passed”**, not “unpaid,” “missed,” or an alert. This is an honest calendar fact; payment state remains unknown.

### Upcoming integration

The overview loads detected streams and own active obligations under the same request scope, projects the two concepts independently, then merges the resulting occurrences for one sort and one set of totals.

Use a source-discriminated occurrence type:

- `source: "detected"` retains account/currency/direction/normalized-name/cadence identity.
- `source: "obligation"` carries the stable `obligationId` and always has direction `outflow`.

Do not invent a normalized merchant identity for an obligation. Calendar/list keys for known items use `obligationId` plus occurrence date; detected items keep their structural stream identity.

Map a stored obligation amount `N` to Upcoming `amountMinor = -N`. Sum it into the existing negative `toLeaveMinor`; do not net it against `toArriveMinor`. Keep each currency separate.

Stable combined ordering is:

1. occurrence date ascending;
2. display name ascending;
3. account ID ascending;
4. source ascending;
5. source-specific stable identity ascending.

Detected-stream behavior is otherwise unchanged: tracked status, one-overdue rule, stale threshold, deposits, Gone quiet section, and history links retain their current semantics. Explicit obligations never enter Gone quiet.

## Overlap and double-counting policy

### Approved Phase One policy: show both and warn

When a known occurrence and detected occurrence have the same:

- `accountId`;
- `currency`;
- `date`; and
- absolute amount magnitude,

mark them as a **possible overlap**. This exact structural comparison does not use merchant-string heuristics and does not mutate, link, hide, or settle either source.

Both occurrences remain visible and both remain in totals. Show `Known` and `Predicted` badges plus concise copy such as:

> Possible overlap — both are counted until matching is available.

Why this policy was selected:

- Equality is not identity proof; two legitimate charges can share all four fields.
- Silently suppressing money is the more dangerous failure in a financial forecast.
- Actual Budget’s duplicate/merge history demonstrates that matching needs its own evidence and repair model.
- Cash Lens explicitly assigns matching to 3.5.2 and prohibits brittle matching heuristics.

The warning is advisory only. The person can end the declaration or dismiss the detected recurring stream through its existing surface if they know one source is wrong.

### Rejected alternatives at this scope

- **Explicit wins:** suppress the detected occurrence and its total on an exact overlap. This gives a cleaner headline but can silently undercount two real charges.
- **Manual link now:** add a relation between an obligation and detected stream. It avoids guessing but pulls identity, lifecycle, link editing, and future-history behavior from 3.5.2 into this leaf.

The founder approved the show-both policy and required account assignment.

## UI design

Keep the feature inside `/upcoming`; do not add a seventh mobile-navigation item.

### Known obligations management

Add a **Known obligations** section that lists every non-ended declaration, including one whose next date is beyond the displayed month. It is the management source of truth; the month calendar remains a projection.

An “Add obligation” control opens a compact form with:

- Name.
- Account (required).
- Amount.
- Currency, defaulted from the selected account.
- First due date.
- Repeat: Once, Weekly, Every two weeks, Monthly, Yearly.
- Optional final date, shown only for repeating choices.

Each card shows name, formatted positive charge amount, account, human-readable schedule, next date when one exists, and Edit / End actions. If no future date remains but a final occurrence is still passed, say so plainly. End uses an inline confirmation naming the obligation; it does not offer hard delete.

Server validation is authoritative. Mutation controls reuse the repository’s established pending, generic-error, and refresh behavior. Focus returns to a useful control after close/success; errors are textual as well as visual.

### Calendar and chronological lists

- Preserve the existing semantic calendar `<table>`, caption, and scoped weekday headers.
- Show a compact `Known` badge on obligation occurrences and `Predicted` on detected occurrences.
- Render “Scheduled date passed” for a known passed occurrence. Keep existing detected-stream wording for detected overdue items.
- A known occurrence must not link as though an actual ledger transaction exists. Its action targets the declaration’s management/edit affordance; detected rows retain transaction-history drill-through.
- Possible-overlap text identifies that both rows are counted.
- Source and warning meaning cannot rely on color alone.

At approximately 320 CSS pixels, fields, actions, cards, totals, and calendar must remain within the viewport. Stack controls when needed; no body-level horizontal overflow. The calendar may use its existing contained overflow strategy if necessary.

### Empty and unavailable states

- No accounts: explain that an account is required and link to Accounts; do not present an unusable create form.
- Accounts but no declarations: show a short example-driven empty state (rent, insurance, tuition) and Add action.
- No occurrences in the current projection month: management still shows active declarations beyond the month.
- Ended declarations are retained in storage but no ended-history UI is added in 3.5.1.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Known and detected sources double-count one real charge | Visible source badges, exact non-destructive overlap warning, explicit “both counted” copy; matching deferred rather than guessed |
| Suppression hides two legitimate equal charges | Approved policy never suppresses or mutates either source |
| A passed date is mistaken for proof of nonpayment | Say only “Scheduled date passed”; no paid/missed label or alert |
| Old schedules make projection unbounded | Jump by cadence arithmetic to the reference neighborhood; loop only through the bounded visible window |
| Month-end recurrence drifts | Preserve the original `starts_on` anchor; exhaustive clamp/leap tests |
| Cross-user account or obligation access | Derived identity, explicit owner predicates, forced RLS, composite FK, byte-identical 404s, dual-user and raw-role tests |
| Money loses precision or sign meaning | Reuse `parseMajorUnits`, enforce safe positive minor units, map to outflow sign once at projection |
| Account purge silently removes declarations | Cascade deliberately and disclose affected known obligations in destructive confirmation |
| Early schema makes 3.5.2 harder | Stable obligation ID and same-owner unique key; no speculative occurrence/payment schema to unwind |
| New dependency or provider expands attack surface | Add neither |

## Explicit deferrals

- Transaction/obligation matching and durable matched identity.
- Paid, partial, missing, late, duplicate, and amount-variance state.
- Alerts, notifications, email, push, cron, queues, and reminders.
- Materialized occurrence rows or future ledger transactions.
- Expected income and scheduled transfers.
- Optional or unassigned accounts.
- Categories, merchants, notes, attachments, and documents.
- Pause/restore/end-history UI and hard delete.
- Semimonthly, multiple dates per month, nth weekday, last business day, every-N units, weekend/holiday movement, arbitrary RRULEs, and calendar export.
- Approximate/ranged amounts and inflation/escalation rules.
- Balance forecasting, FX conversion, and a combined base-currency total.
- User-timezone definition of today and month-navigation UI beyond the existing validated `?on` seam.
- Provider-side bill cancellation/payment and new provider scopes.
