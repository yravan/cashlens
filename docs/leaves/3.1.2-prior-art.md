# 3.1.2 Balance History — Prior Art

Survey date: 2026-09-16

Status: Adopted design approved by the founder on 2026-09-16.

Scope: daily account-balance history, provider freshness, logical-day and event-time semantics, corrections, idempotency, missed days, bounded backfill, retention, manual accounts, multi-currency, serverless triggering, recovery, concurrency, and the account-level contract needed by future net-worth consumers.

## Terms used in this survey

- **Current projection**: the one mutable balance currently stored for an account. Cash Lens already has this in `account_balances`.
- **Observation**: a balance Cash Lens actually received from a provider or person, with an exact instant when available.
- **Daily snapshot**: the one account/day value retained for historical consumers. It may be corrected by a later, better observation on the same day.
- **Carry-forward**: a read-time value copied from the latest earlier snapshot to render a continuous series. It is not a new observation.
- **Freshness**: how old the underlying observation is. Request time, provider balance time, provider event time, capture time, and logical snapshot day are not interchangeable.

## Findings

### Current state and historical state are different models

Cash Lens's `account_balances` is keyed only by `account_id`; provider sync and manual re-anchoring overwrite it. It is a current-state projection and cannot represent more than one day. Production systems that retain history use a separate account/day relation or derive history from a complete authoritative ledger. Cash Lens needs the separate relation because provider balances contain institution state that the transaction ledger cannot reconstruct reliably.

The current projection remains useful for the Accounts page and as the source of an uncaptured observation. Historical consumers should not query it as if it were a series.

### Freshness and time need separate fields

A daily row needs at least two independent time concepts:

1. a logical `date` identifying the point in the daily series; and
2. an exact `timestamptz` identifying when the underlying balance was observed.

Capture time is separate again when an earlier current observation is written to history during reconciliation. A provider-reported timestamp must remain nullable and must never be synthesized from request time. For manual accounts, `reported_on` is a user-supplied local date while `as_of` is the server instant; both matter.

A `timestamptz` alone cannot preserve user-local or institution-local day identity. If the product chooses user-local days, the IANA zone must be stored and conversion must be explicit. Fixed offsets are insufficient across daylight-saving transitions.

### Corrected daily rows are a better MVP than an immutable event log

Maybe and Ghostfolio materialize one corrected row per account/day. This bounds storage, makes range reads simple, and matches the leaf's promise of daily snapshots. An immutable observation log would preserve every intraday event but would require a second materialized daily model, correction-selection rules, more retention policy, and more consumer complexity.

The daily key must be unique. A later observation may replace the row only when it is demonstrably newer or more authoritative; an identical replay is a no-op. `created_at` and `updated_at` retain first-capture and correction timing without pretending to be a full audit log.

### A gap is not zero and not a hidden observation

No row means no observation for that day. It must never become zero. A range consumer may carry the latest earlier value forward, but the returned point needs `carried: true` and the original observation instant so callers can disclose staleness. Carry-forward belongs in reads, not storage.

A fresh provider call that reports an unchanged amount is still a genuine new observation and may create that day's row. Merely reading yesterday's stale current projection is not a new observation and must not create today's row.

### Provider history cannot be safely reverse-engineered

Plaid Accounts and SimpleFIN expose current snapshots, not a complete historical balance series. Reversing current balance through transactions assumes complete activity, correct posting dates, no holds, no interest or fees outside the feed, and no institution adjustments. Those assumptions are not defensible. Existing accounts can receive one bootstrap observation using the current row's original `as_of`; the system must not spread that amount across earlier days.

Manual accounts are different: their approved anchor-plus-ledger rule defines the displayed balance. Cash Lens can derive effective values from the anchor and posted transactions, provided the raw user-entered anchor remains distinguishable and recomputation is deterministic.

### Multi-currency and account signs belong at the consumer boundary

A historical amount is meaningful only with its currency. Different currencies stay in separate series until an explicit, dated FX source exists. Stored provider/manual amounts preserve current Cash Lens semantics: held-account positive values mean held; credit/loan positive values mean owed. The future 6.3.2 consumer applies asset/liability factors during aggregation rather than mutating stored signs.

## Trigger comparison

| **Sync/manual-event capture** | Every successful initial provider registration, fresh balance refresh, and manual re-anchor can atomically write history. | No row on days with no fresh event. Transaction webhook activity does not prove balance freshness. | Reuses authenticated/provider-verified paths and owner-scoped RLS. Smallest new surface. | **Adopt.** It is the authoritative write seam. |
| **Signed-in read-time capture** | Can reconcile a current observation that a prior path failed to write. | Depends on user presence. Must key the observation to its original day and must not stamp a stale value as newly observed today. | Reuses normal auth/RLS. | **Platform-native Cron route** | Can attempt wall-clock work without user presence. | Best-effort, UTC, duplicates, misses, overlap, no failed-invocation retry; Hobby daily timing is coarse. | Public authenticated endpoint plus a way to enumerate owners despite current RLS. | **Bounded hybrid** | Events create genuine observations; reads repair missed writes; optional scheduled reconciliation later closes inactive-user gaps. | Storage honestly shows gaps until a genuine observation exists. | Starts with existing trust boundaries and leaves the scheduler as an explicit security decision. | **Adopted without the optional scheduler.** |

## Background-job risk and required mitigation

A platform-wide worker is not a neutral reliability improvement. Today `cashlens_app` cannot enumerate every user's connection under RLS. Enabling a global sweep therefore creates a new cross-tenant execution capability. The dangerous shortcut is an owner, superuser, `BYPASSRLS`, or broadly privileged runtime URL; compromise would expose every account and encrypted credential row at once.

If wall-clock daily capture is approved, the minimum defensible shape is:

- an authenticated Cron starter that accepts only Vercel's `Authorization: Bearer <CRON_SECRET>` and returns indistinguishably for unauthorized requests;
- a dedicated dispatcher privilege that can enumerate only opaque owner/connection identifiers, never balances, names, amounts, transaction data, or credential ciphertext;
- one bounded per-owner or per-connection task entering the ordinary request scope before reading user data;
- encrypted provider tokens exposed only at the exact provider-call seam, never serialized into workflow state, queue messages, browser responses, logs, fixtures, or test output;
- an account/day unique key, guarded UPSERT, and transaction-level advisory lock or equivalent cooperating lock;
- fixed limits on owners, connections, provider calls, retries, reconciliation days, and concurrent consumers;
- retries with explicit permanent/transient classification and a terminal failed-work view because transport delivery is not exactly once;
- logs containing opaque ids, counts, durations, and sanitized error classes only—never names, balances, amounts, payloads, or tokens;
- alerts for missing scheduled starts, failed/expired work, unusual duplicate rates, and stale last-success timestamps;
- no provider call solely to manufacture a row when the provider cannot promise fresh data.

A scheduled design still cannot promise one genuine provider observation every calendar day. It can promise attempted reconciliation and honest freshness metadata.

## Adopted pattern

- Keep `account_balances` as the mutable current projection and add a separate user-owned account/day history relation.
- Retain one corrected daily value per account, enforced by a unique key and guarded atomic UPSERT.
- Store the logical day, amount, currency, exact observation time, capture/correction timestamps, and enough provenance to distinguish provider observation, manual anchor/effective value, and bootstrap/catch-up.
- Preserve a genuine provider timestamp only when supplied; otherwise label the Cash Lens retrieval instant honestly.
- Capture on successful provider connection/refresh and manual re-anchor; use signed-in reads only to reconcile an already-existing uncaptured observation.
- Keep missing storage days missing. Carry forward only in a range-read contract and mark every carried point.
- Store no zero for unavailable current balance and no row when there is no usable current value.
- Keep currencies separate and stored signs unchanged; leave net-worth aggregation and charts to 6.3.2.
- Retain snapshots with the account until account/user purge.
- Do not add a platform-wide background role in the approved MVP.

## Rejected or deferred

- Reusing `account_balances` as history: its account primary key and overwrite semantics make this impossible.
- Treating transaction sync/webhooks as proof of a fresh balance.
- Reverse-backfilling provider history from transactions or copying today's value into earlier dates.
- Persisting carry-forward rows, converting missing values to zero, or silently treating a stale read as a fresh observation.
- Implicit FX, cross-currency totals, and net-worth computation (6.3.2).
- An immutable intraday observation ledger plus a separate materializer: useful for audit-heavy products, disproportionate to this daily-history leaf.
- Storing every available/limit change unless the product explicitly expands the leaf beyond the current balance needed for trends and net worth.
- An owner/superuser/`BYPASSRLS` cron connection.
- Treating Vercel Cron, Workflow, queue deduplication, or advisory locks as exactly-once guarantees.
