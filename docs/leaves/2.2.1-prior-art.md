# 2.2.1 Manual Transactions — Prior Art

Survey date: 2026-09-11

Scope: how production personal-finance products and open-source ledgers let a person record activity a feed cannot see; which account, amount, direction, date, description, merchant, and category fields they expose; how manual entries coexist with imported rows and balances; edit/delete authority; reconciliation and transfer interactions; exact-money and security implications.

The survey covers 37 production, open-source, or official-documentation sources across YNAB, Monarch Money, Actual Budget, Firefly III, Lunch Money, Copilot Money, Plaid, GnuCash, hledger, and Beancount.

## Adopted patterns

1. **One canonical ledger.** Manual and imported rows share `transactions`; `source` records provenance. Every downstream consumer sees one model.
2. **Manual entry fills feed gaps.** Any existing owned account can receive a manual row. Requiring an offline account first would make 2.2.1 unusable before 2.2.3.
3. **Direction, then unsigned amount.** The form asks Money out or Money in and accepts a positive decimal. The server applies Cash Lens's sign convention.
4. **Account-authoritative denomination.** Currency comes from the selected account, never the request. Account reassignment updates currency atomically and performs no FX conversion.
5. **Immediate ledger fact.** A manual transaction is `posted`, because there is no provider settlement lifecycle to await.
6. **Complete correction of user-authored rows.** Date, account, direction/amount, description, merchant, and category can be corrected. Source, status, source ID, currency, and ownership cannot be submitted.
7. **Provider authority.** Plaid/import rows are indistinguishable from unknown rows to this mutation surface.
8. **Hard delete with confirmation.** A manual row has no upstream source that can restore it. Dependent transfer/proposal rows follow existing foreign-key cascades.
9. **Separate transaction and balance authority.** CRUD never writes account balances.
10. **Existing enrichment and reporting paths.** Categories, auto-categorization, transfer matching, recurring detection, cash flow, and spending consume the canonical row rather than manual-only branches.
11. **Exact boundary validation.** Submitted money stays a decimal string until exact minor-unit conversion under the account's currency exponent.
12. **Narrow first release.** Single-row CRUD only; reconciliation, bulk editing, imports, manual accounts, annotations, and cross-source deduplication remain their own leaves.

## Rejected patterns

- **A separate manual-transactions table.** It would duplicate RLS, indexes, foreign keys, pagination, and every downstream aggregation, and force union logic throughout the app.
- **Editing every source through one generic endpoint.** Provider modifications would overwrite facts Plaid is expected to refresh and would pre-empt 3.2.2's durable override model.
- **Auto-creating a Cash Wallet.** Account creation and maintained balances are 2.2.3. Hidden creation would make ownership and balance semantics surprising.
- **Changing balances from transaction CRUD.** Current connected-account balances are provider facts; transaction-derived or periodically maintained balances need their own explicit model.
- **Signed free-form amount input.** A sign plus an inflow/outflow selector creates contradictory states. Direction plus a non-zero unsigned decimal has one meaning.
- **Caller-supplied currency.** It could create a transaction whose denomination disagrees with its account and bypass safe precision rules.
- **Pending manual transactions.** There is no provider authorization/settlement transition. Scheduled future obligations belong to 3.5.1.
- **Soft deletion.** There is no reimport source to suppress, no audit-history promise in this leaf, and the UI already requires explicit confirmation.
- **Automatic purchase deduplication.** Matching a future Plaid row to a manually entered purchase is 3.3.2; this leaf only invokes existing internal-transfer matching.
- **Double-entry or transfer-specific entry UI.** Users may enter both real account legs; 3.3.1 already pairs eligible posted rows. A special transfer authoring model is separate product scope.
- **Bulk operations and reconciliation.** Both are mature workflows, but neither is necessary to fulfill add/edit/delete by hand.

## Distilled design

The proven common denominator is a manual row in the ordinary account register, entered with explicit direction and exact amount, correctable because the user authored it, and kept separate from balance reconciliation. Cash Lens applies that pattern to its current architecture: canonical `transactions`, strict source authority, account-derived currency, category provenance preservation, active-transfer consistency, RLS-scoped mutations, and no balance side effect.
