# 6.2.1 Spending by category — prior-art survey

The leaf: totals per category for any period, drillable to the underlying transactions — the
"understand groceries and health in detail" view. Survey questions: how do production
personal-finance systems present category spending; how do group and leaf totals relate; how is
the period chosen; where do uncategorized rows, refunds, and income go; and what gets excluded.

## Sources

Commercial products

1. Monarch Money — [Using Reports](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/21846787088916-Using-Reports):
   the Spending tab renders the same totals as pie, breakdown, trend bars, or treemap, grouped
   by category, category group, or merchant; every chart element clicks through to the
   transactions behind it; filters cover timeframe, category, merchant, account, and amount.
2. Monarch practice — [FPFoCo's cash-flow guide](https://www.financialplanningfortcollins.com/blog/monarch-money-fpfoco-cash-flow-planning/):
   refunds are categorized to the original expense category so the credit nets against the
   category's spending instead of inflating income; income categories are reserved for money
   from third parties.
3. Copilot Money — [Categories Tab Overview](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/9504513-categories-tab-overview):
   categories are listed sorted by spend amount; "money you earn is considered income, and
   income transactions aren't included in your spending budgets"; the catch-all "Other"
   category cannot be deleted — uncategorized activity always has a visible home.
4. Copilot Money — [Refund and Reimbursement Transactions](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/5325170-refund-and-reimbursement-transactions):
   a refund categorized into the original purchase's category reduces that category's spend;
   mixed deposits are split so the reimbursed portion lands in the right category.
5. YNAB — [Spending Breakdown](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/spending-breakdown-H1H7YxmD0):
   categories shown by spending share, with an explicit affordance to "identify categories
   with a greater inflow than outflow" — refund-dominant categories are surfaced, not hidden;
   drill-down runs group → category → transactions.
6. YNAB — [Income v Expense](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/income-v-expense-Byu1BYWRq):
   income sits in its own report section (green heading up top) apart from expense categories —
   direction separation is a presentation concern, handled per report.
7. Quicken Simplifi — [Using Reports](https://support.simplifi.quicken.com/en/articles/4592676-using-reports-in-quicken-simplifi):
   the Spending report "shows expenses only"; breakdowns by category, account, tag, or payee
   with Summary and Transactions views; clicking a parent category reveals its subcategories
   with totals and the underlying transactions; the graph caps at the top 11 categories plus
   "Everything Else"; any date range, saveable as a named report.
8. Quicken Simplifi — [Spending Report](https://info.quicken.com/sim/spending-report):
   recommends comparing full months to full months, not partial periods; filter + range
   combinations are worth persisting — the report's state is its definition.
9. Empower Personal Dashboard — [Dashboard](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/201169740-Dashboard-Overview) /
   [Transactions Overview](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/201169690-Transactions-Overview):
   the expense view lists categories sorted by amount and clicks through to that category's
   transactions; uncategorized rows are isolated via the category filter to be cleaned up —
   findable, never silently dropped.
10. Lunch Money — [Overview](https://support.lunchmoney.app/home/overview): the Spending
    Breakdown card runs off a period picker — "This month" by default, presets down to a fully
    custom date range; exclude-from-totals categories are omitted per an explicit display
    setting, never silently.
11. Mint (retired) — practitioner guides ([Ford Financial](https://www.fordfinancialsolutions.com/blog/2017/02/07/best-practices-for-using-mint),
    [Abacus Wealth](https://abacuswealth.com/five-mint-problems-and-their-solutions/)):
    Trends-by-category over a chosen timeframe; transfers had to be hand-labeled out of trends;
    the "Everything Else" bucket caught the remainder. The migration threads to Simplifi ask
    for the same controls — the breakdown table + drill is the retained core.

Open source

12. Firefly III — [Reports docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/how-to/firefly-iii/finances/reports/):
    the category report shows spent and earned per category over the selected period — both
    directions kept distinct, not collapsed.
13. Firefly III — [discussion #10935](https://github.com/orgs/firefly-iii/discussions/10935):
    the category report excludes uncategorized transactions entirely; users call it misleading —
    "the report's total should match the full period amount … otherwise money appears to be
    missing," and there is no way to see how much money remains uncategorized. The maintainer's
    "the category report is for transactions with categories" is the anti-pattern this leaf
    avoids.
14. Firefly III — [issue #8345](https://github.com/firefly-iii/firefly-iii/issues/8345): a
    category endpoint that reports only a sum is insufficient — spent and earned must be
    distinguishable per category.
15. Maybe Finance — [`income_statement.rb`](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/blob/main/app/models/income_statement.rb):
    the totals iteration runs over every category *plus a synthetic uncategorized category*;
    a parent's total is its direct rows plus its children's rollup; the period total sums
    top-level entries only (no double count); transfers cannot be categorized and are excluded
    by the transaction scope itself.
16. Maybe Finance — [issue #1837](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/issues/1837) and
    [PR #1730](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe/pull/1730): subcategories not adding up
    to their parents is treated as a bug; nesting is capped at one level to keep rollups exact —
    the same one-level taxonomy 4.1.1 chose.
17. Actual Budget — [Custom Reports](https://actualbudget.org/docs/reports/custom-reports/):
    report types are Payment (outflows), Deposit (inflows), and Net — "sum of outflows and
    inflows," the figures "shown in Budget page under Spent and Received columns"; splits by
    Category or Group; uncategorized rows appear via "Show uncategorized"; ranges are Live
    ("This month", updates itself) or Static (fixed dates).

## What everyone agrees on

- Category spending is its own destination — a Reports/Categories/Stats surface (Monarch,
  Copilot, Simplifi, YNAB, Lunch Money) — not a dashboard widget.
- A category's total nets its inflows: refunds categorized into a category reduce it (Monarch,
  Copilot, YNAB); sum-only APIs that can't distinguish spent from earned are a documented gap
  (Firefly #8345, Actual's Spent/Received columns).
- Groups roll up leaves exactly; non-additivity is a bug (Maybe #1837), and parent rows expand
  into subcategory totals in place (Simplifi, YNAB).
- Every total drills to its transactions (Monarch, Simplifi, YNAB, Empower).
- The period is user-chosen — presets plus a custom range (Lunch Money, Simplifi, Actual).
- Uncategorized activity stays visible (Copilot's undeletable Other, Actual's toggle, Maybe's
  synthetic category, Empower's cleanup flow); dropping it is the documented failure mode
  (Firefly #10935).
- Internal transfers are not spending and are excluded structurally where possible (Maybe makes
  them uncategorizable; Mint's hand-labeling is the cautionary tale — full survey in
  6.1.1-prior-art.md).
- Rows sort by spend, largest first (Copilot, Empower).

## Adopted

- **Own route, `/spending`, in the primary nav.** 6.2 is a breakdown family (merchants 6.2.2
  and trends 6.2.3 will join it); every surveyed product gives this view its own surface.
- **The 6.1.1 true-spend law, verbatim**: posted rows only, active transfer-pair members
  excluded whatever their category; the excluded-leg count and the pending count (scoped to
  the filtered period) are disclosed in the same words the dashboard uses.
- **Spent and received kept distinct per category, net displayed** (Firefly's spent/earned,
  Actual's Spent/Received/Net): each row shows its net; a row with flow in both directions
  additionally discloses the split, so a refund inside Travel reads as what it is instead of
  silently shrinking a number.
- **One list per currency, every active category, sorted net-ascending (most spent first),
  income landing last with a plus sign** — Actual's Net-split-by-Category shape rather than
  the hide-income convention (Simplifi/Copilot/YNAB). Two reasons this beats the majority:
  the taxonomy has no income/expense type column to filter on (and inventing one is 4.1.x
  territory), and hiding net-positive categories recreates Firefly #10935 — period totals that
  don't reconcile and refund-dominant categories that vanish (the case YNAB explicitly
  surfaces). Per-currency section totals (in/out/net over the period) sit above the rows, so
  the breakdown visibly sums to the same numbers 6.1.1 reports.
- **Group rollup = direct rows + leaf rows** (Maybe's `income_statement` exactly): the app only
  assigns leaves, but the math stays correct even for a row that reaches a parent id through
  some future path; leaf rows render under their group, Simplifi-style.
- **Uncategorized as an honest final row, always drillable**: the one extension to the 3.6.1
  URL schema this design demands — `category=uncategorized` filtering the history page to
  `category_id IS NULL` (red-first; Actual's toggle made the default, Maybe's synthetic
  category as a URL value).
- **Period = the 3.6.1 `from`/`to`/`currency` grammar on the URL, closed schema**: only those
  three keys are legal on `/spending`; anything else — including valid history params like
  `q` — rejects before SQL, same as the transactions page. No params = all time, derived from
  the data with no clock read (the 6.1.1 determinism rationale; Actual's Static range).
- **Per-currency sections, no FX** (repo law since 6.1.1/6.3.1).
- **A textual, accessible table** — Monarch's four chart types all reduce to the breakdown
  list; charts add color-only meaning and nothing the promise needs.

## Rejected

- **Hiding income/net-positive categories** (Simplifi's expenses-only report, Copilot, YNAB) —
  see above; the ledger-first thesis says every dollar is accounted for.
- **Pie/treemap/bar charts** (Monarch, Lunch Money) — presentation sugar; trends and
  comparisons are 6.2.3's promise.
- **Top-N + "Everything Else" collapsing** (Simplifi's 11-series cap, Mint) — a chart-legend
  constraint; a table shows every active category.
- **Budget-pace coloring** (Copilot's green/yellow/red bars) — 6.6.1 category targets.
- **Current-period default with presets** (Lunch Money's "This month", Empower, Actual's Live
  ranges) — needs a clock; the deterministic default is all time, and the from/to form plus
  shareable URLs cover any period. Client-side presets can layer on later without touching the
  contract.
- **Group-id drill into the transactions page** (`category=<groupId>` matching children,
  Simplifi's parent click) — the leaf breakdown is already visible in place under each group
  row; widening the 3.6.1 category filter to a subquery is deferred until a view needs it.
- **Merchant grouping** (Monarch's third axis) — 6.2.2.
- **Saved named reports** (Simplifi) — the URL is the saved report; a persistence surface adds
  nothing at MVP.
- **FX conversion to a base currency** — repo law; unlike currencies never combine.
